# The Pick Team trial: three directions, as token values

Written before drawing (`docs/design/foundations.md`, The trial fills them, step 2). Each answers the
approved look statement: a broadcast desk, square, ruled and dense, on a neutral ground; navy and gold
from the LCK; Hangul in Pretendard beside a Latin face that is not Inter; one object per screen.
Pick Team's object is the Rift. All three draw the same data (`data.js`) and the same content and order
as `approved/pick-team/`: buffs over the Rift, the bench under it, the bar with Save, the tapped
player's card and the week's series on the right.

## Would I do this for any similar page?

Asked of each choice before drawing; what is left is what makes it this game's page.

1. **Desk.** A warm grey ground is common on any dashboard: kept only because the navy Rift is the one
   block of colour on it, so the object carries the screen. Archivo at a wide setting for heads is not a
   default; the hangul at full title size in navy is the LCK's bilingual setting, not a footnote.
2. **Broadcast.** Dark grounds are the default "esports" answer: so the ground is neutral black, not
   navy or slate, and the navy is spent only where a broadcast spends it, the lower thirds (each tile's
   band, the card's name). Barlow's condensed cut is a scoreboard face, chosen over a generic geometric.
   The 44px hangul beside the 28px title is DesignStudio's LCK move: scale against scale.
3. **Board.** White ground and black 2px rules are a newspaper's sports page. The one navy panel makes
   the buffs, the Rift and the bench one object, which no generic page would group. Red Hat's mono gives
   the figures a family face, not a borrowed code font.

Directions 1 and 3 share a light ground, so they differ in more than colour: row height, Rift size,
what is grouped into the object and how the hangul is set.
