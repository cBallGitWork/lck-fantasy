# The LCK brand layer: what makes Flol feel Korean and LCK, not Western-with-Korean-words

Read 18 Nov 2026, by search and fetch only. Builds on, and does not repeat, `docs/research/lck-visual-research.md` (LCK palette from Namu, LoL client colours, ten 2026 teams and kits, portrait rights, Riot's Legal Jibber Jabber), `lck-team-logos-and-memes.md`, `lec-team-*.md`, `esports-fantasy-design-ideas.md` and `docs/design/plate.md`. Where this file corrects those, it says so.

Context: Flol wants one core design (layout, components, density, type scale) and a brand layer per region. This file is the LCK brand layer's evidence.

## 1. Who made the LCK look, and what it is built on

### Takeaway
The 2021 LCK identity was made by DesignStudio (London), the same agency as the LEC's 2019 rebrand; its signature is Hangul and English headlines woven together using scale, contrast and space, on simplified shapes and sky-inspired colour. This corrects the repo's "Agency: not found".

### Cited Findings
- The LCK revealed its 2021 rebrand "in association with DesignStudio"; creative director Elise Santangelo is quoted on it. — [Esports Insider](https://esportsinsider.com/2021/01/lck-partners-with-designstudio-to-unleash-new-rebrand/) (search snippet; the page returned 403 to fetch)
- Design description: DesignStudio "confidently weave Hangul and English headlines together, using techniques of scale, contrast and space to create a sense of awe"; the identity uses "simplified shapes and bright colors, inspired by the sky and the atmosphere". — [Design Week, Feb 2021](https://www.designweek.co.uk/issues/7-13-february-2021/esports-branding) (snippet; page 403 to fetch); [Inven Global](https://www.invenglobal.com/articles/12982/the-new-era-of-lck-is-here-a-visual-journey-into-the-new-broadcast-and-lol-park)
- Santangelo, per Design Week, said the Korean league presented the "richest heritage" among Riot's leagues. — [Design Week](https://www.designweek.co.uk/issues/7-13-february-2021/esports-branding) (snippet)
- The LEC's 2019 rebrand is also DesignStudio's. — [Creative Boom](https://www.creativeboom.com/work/designstudio-and-riot-games-rebrand-of-the-league-of-legends-european-championship/); [It's Nice That](https://www.itsnicethat.com/news/designstudio-league-of-legends-rebrand-graphic-design-211118)
- The LCK Cup 2025 opening title was made by Korean studio Super Very More, working with Riot Games Korea to bring in each team's narratives and symbols. — [Marvelous Designer spotlight](https://www.marvelousdesigner.com/learn/userspotlight/post/2aa0a767e6c0464c80b132d6a403f49a)
- Banana Culture produced AR graphics for LCK broadcasts and finals (2020 to 2024 seasons listed in its portfolio). — [Banana Culture, 2024 Summer Finals](https://bananaculture.asia/2024-lck-summer-finals-opening-ceremony-ar)
- From 2026 to 2030, Naver (CHZZK) and SOOP are the exclusive Korean platforms; LoL Park becomes "CHZZK LoL Park"; CHZZK adds GRID live data. — [Inven Global](https://www.invenglobal.com/datalab/articles/19982/lck-secures-landmark-20262030-partnership-with-naver-and-soop-rebrands-lol-park-and-locks-in-exclusive-broadcast-deal); [byline.network](https://byline.network/2025/12/16_1928873/)

### Inferences
- The LCK and LEC looks share a parent agency and a system logic (one mark, a boxy sans, bold scale contrast). That is evidence that "one core, a regional skin" is how Riot itself runs its leagues; Flol's split mirrors it.
- The LCK's own distinctive move is bilingual typography as a design element: Hangul and Latin set together at contrasting scales. Flol's plate already does a small version (title + hangul; name 40 + hangul 20). The LCK layer should push this further, not add ornaments.
- Production of LCK broadcast motion has moved to Korean studios (Super Very More, Banana Culture). There is no published 2026 overlay spec.

## 2. The broadcasters' brands that frame LCK viewing (CHZZK, SOOP)

### Takeaway
Korean LCK viewers watch inside two platform brands: CHZZK (Naver green on near-black, a tilted "de-squared" Hangul wordmark) and SOOP (blue/green, rebranded from AfreecaTV in Oct 2024). Both are trademarks; borrow the habits (dark ground, one neon accent, playful game-slang UI words), never the marks.

### Cited Findings
- CHZZK: Naver's green set on a black ground ("네이버를 상징하는 녹색을 검은 바탕을 중심으로"); wordmark is a 탈네모꼴 (non-square-frame) face with 치 and 직 bent into a lightning bolt; the display face across the service is Sandoll Nemony (산돌 네모니); likes and dislikes are labelled 버프/너프 (buff/nerf). — [Design Compass](https://designcompass.org/2023/12/20/chzzk/)
- CHZZK's brand guide forbids altering the logo's form or colour and forbids commercial use. — [CHZZK brand guides](https://chzzk.gitbook.io/chzzk/resources/brand-guides) (via search summary)
- CHZZK token values, third-party transcription of chzzk.naver.com CSS: neon green `#00FFA3` on dark `#141517`; light-theme green `#1BB373`. — [oppadu design-system gallery](https://www.oppadu.com/tools/design-systems-site/brand/chzzk.html) (third party, unverified against the guide)
- SOOP: AfreecaTV renamed the company in March 2024 and the Korean service on 15 Oct 2024; the mark is an infinity-like "OO"; corporate "Essential Blue" (inherited from AfreecaTV), global "Energy Green", domestic "Infinity Blue"; the mascot was dropped and "BJ" became "streamer". — [Design Compass](https://designcompass.org/2024/10/17/soop/); [byline.network](https://byline.network/2024/03/29-257/)
- SOOP's esports page revamp (2026) foregrounds live, VOD and 승부예측 (match prediction), with nearly 2,000 prediction events expected in the year. — [Inven Global](https://www.invenglobal.com/articles/25427/soop-revamps-esports-page-to-expand-amateur-engagement); [My Daily](https://www.mydaily.co.kr/page/view/2026090111203230427)
- DN Freecs became DN SOOPers (team blue is SOOP's blue). — [Smart Today](https://www.smarttoday.co.kr/ko-kr/articles/99513)

### Inferences
- "Dark ground plus one saturated accent" is the shared Korean streaming look. Flol's dark navy plus one accent already fits; the accent should not be CHZZK green or SOOP blue, which would read as a platform tie-in.
- Game slang as UI labels (buff/nerf for like/dislike) is a Korean platform habit worth copying in the voice layer: Flol's Cheer/Jeer, buffs and Price changes are in that spirit.
- Prediction (승부예측) and cheering (응원) are expected features on Korean esports pages; Flol's per-match surfaces could show a two-colour split bar, a familiar Korean pattern.

### Gaps
- No official CHZZK or SOOP hex read from a first-party page.
- No first-hand article on Naver Sports' LCK page layout found.

## 3. Korean product typography: what Korean apps actually use

### Takeaway
Korean product UI runs on a gothic (sans) with tight, slightly negative tracking, a body around 15 to 17px and 1.5 leading. Pretendard (OFL) is the de facto free standard and the government's KRDS mandates a Pretendard cut; big apps commission their own (Toss Product Sans). Flol's plate uses IBM Plex Sans KR, which is good but reads more "global corporate" than "Korean app".

### Cited Findings
- Pretendard is built on Inter (Latin), Source Han Sans (Hangul) and M PLUS 1p (kana); 9 weights plus variable; SIL OFL; recommended stack `"Pretendard Variable", Pretendard, -apple-system, BlinkMacSystemFont, system-ui`; dynamic subset loads only needed slices. — [Pretendard README](https://cdn.jsdelivr.net/npm/pretendard@1.3.9/docs/en/README.md)
- Pretendard was started to cut the extra tracking work Source Han-style gothics needed in UI. — [Design Compass](https://designcompass.org/en/2021/07/05-1tp-3tea/pretendard/); [GitHub](https://github.com/orioncactus/pretendard)
- The Korean government design system (KRDS) requires "Pretendard GOV". — [KRDS](https://www.krds.go.kr/html/eng/outline/outline_02.html)
- KRDS body default 17px / 400 / 1.5 leading (third-party transcription). — [oppadu KRDS](https://www.oppadu.com/tools/design-systems-site/brand/krds.html)
- Toss: its own face, Toss Product Sans (Sandoll; Hangul from Sandoll Gothic Neo1, new Latin and numerals). Scale from public TDS docs: 30/40, 26/35, 22/31, 20/29, body 17/25.5, 15/22.5, caption 13/19.5; tracking -0.025 to -0.04em on headings (marked as estimates); radii 8 to 24px; spacious, one message per section. — [oppadu Toss](https://www.oppadu.com/tools/design-systems-site/brand/toss.html) (third party citing TDS docs; tracking and radii are its estimates)
- Toss designers: "간결한 화면은 다음 행동을 명확하게 안내해 준다" (a simple screen clearly guides the next action); TDS is "one voice" across teams. — [Toss Feed](https://toss.im/tossfeed/article/toss-designer-interview)
- Krafton uses Pretendard as a brand body face. — [Krafton brand](https://brand.krafton.com/Typograhpy)
- Leagues commission display faces: K League's KLeague Dynamic and KLeague Basic (Yoon Design; free for non-commercial use only, and banned for logos/BI); the KFA's KFA Gothic is deliberately narrow for "strength". — [Sandoll Cloud](https://www.sandollcloud.com/free-font/17860/KLeagueDynamic); [Goal Korea](https://www.goal.com/kr/%EB%89%B4%EC%8A%A4/%EB%8C%80%ED%95%9C%EC%B6%95%EA%B5%AC%ED%98%91%ED%9A%8C-19%EB%85%84-%EB%A7%8C%EC%97%90-%EC%83%88-%EC%97%A0%EB%B8%94%EB%9F%BC-%EB%B0%9C%ED%91%9C-bi-%EC%A0%84%EB%A9%B4-%EA%B5%90%EC%B2%B4-goal-live/2yyoy4e3xudo1xxfrsk27zc6f)
- K League's 2021 emblem "Dynamic Pitch" was by Interbrand, stripped of category descriptors. — [Goal Korea](https://www.goal.com/kr/%EB%89%B4%EC%8A%A4/goal-live-k%EB%A6%AC%EA%B7%B8-%EC%8B%A0%EA%B7%9C-%EC%97%A0%EB%B8%94%EB%9F%BC-%EB%B0%8F-%EB%B8%8C%EB%9E%9C%EB%93%9C-%EC%95%84%EC%9D%B4%EB%8D%B4%ED%8B%B0%ED%8B%B0-%EB%8B%A4%EC%9D%B4%EB%82%98%EB%AF%B9-%ED%94%BC%EC%B9%98-%EB%B0%9C%ED%91%9C/1s6uk2qp7581e1ii2hacb2vjq6)

### Inferences
- The tell of "Korean app" type is: a Hangul gothic, tight negative tracking on headings, Hangul and Latin from one family so they match in weight. Western-with-Korean-words usually shows Hangul in a fallback face (Malgun/Apple SD) beside a Western display face, with mismatched weight and default tracking.
- Candidate split for Flol: core type tokens (sizes, weights, line heights) stay in the core; the brand layer names the family and tracking. LCK layer: Pretendard (or keep IBM Plex Sans KR) with -0.02em on headings; a narrow, heavy display face for big figures and names is the sports-league convention (KFA Gothic, K League Dynamic). Free display options already listed in the repo: Gmarket Sans, Black Han Sans. League-owned faces (KLeague) must not be used for a logo and are another league's identity: avoid.
- Plate's IBM Plex Mono for figures has no Korean precedent found; Pretendard has tabular figures, which would keep one family.

## 4. Korean colour habits and density

### Takeaway
Two Korean colour conventions are hard rules, not taste: rises in red and falls in blue (opposite to the West), and team colours as solid blocks. On density, no source measures Korean vs Western apps; the evidence is mixed (portals and stats sites dense; Toss-style fintech spacious).

### Cited Findings
- Korean price screens show 상승 (rise) in red and 하락 (fall) in blue; the US is the reverse (green up). — [StockHub guide](https://stockhub.kr/guide/reading-price-screen)
- T1's creative director: primary colours "red, black, and white"; "In Asia, people really like seeing the players' faces"; one layout serves both languages ("we're not having to make two completely different layouts"); two design teams, North America and Korea, kept "similar in terms of the general branding". — [Inven Global, May Lee interview](https://www.invenglobal.com/lol/articles/17553/meet-the-esports-insiders-6-t1-director-of-creative-brand-strategy-may-lee)
- A 2026 Korean study (8 apps, 58 respondents) found excess features raise perceived UI complexity and lower brand-identity perception, satisfaction and reuse intent; it recommends clear structure over more features. — [earticle](https://www.earticle.net/Article/A483526) (via search summary)
- Korean users are described as used to skimming reviews and dense info quickly. — [Wikitree columnist](https://www.wikitree.co.kr/articles/1145366) (opinion)
- Dense layout as a defining trait is attributed in Korean design press to Chinese apps, contrasted with Korean ones. — [DI Today](https://ditoday.com/?p=99555)

### Inferences
- "Korean density" is not "cram it in". Korean sports and portal pages are table-dense; Korean fintech is spacious. For a stats-heavy fantasy game, dense tables (as the plate already sets: 40px rows, 12px heads) plus Toss-like single-action screens for transactions (Transfers confirm, Save team) is the honest Korean blend.
- Faces matter more in Korea (May Lee), but Flol cannot show official portraits (see lck-visual-research.md §4). The brand layer should compensate with big player game names set like broadcast lower thirds, which the plate already does.
- Red-up/blue-down is the LCK layer's setting. The LEC layer must flip to green-up/red-down (Western market convention) or use neutral arrows: this is a real per-region token, not decoration.

### Gaps
- No measured study of Korean vs Western UI density found.
- No first-hand teardown of KBO or K League apps read.

## 5. What is protected, and the brand-layer split

### Takeaway
Everything distinctive about the LCK's look that is a mark (the two-triangle logo, "We Make Legends", CHZZK/SOOP marks, team crests, league display fonts) is off-limits; what is free is the method: bilingual scale contrast, a cool sky palette near (not equal to) the LCK's, Korean colour conventions, Korean UI type habits, and LCK vocabulary.

### Cited Findings
- Riot's fan policy bars use of its logos and trademarks without a written licence and says it prohibits "the use of our IP in games and apps"; it requires the "Legal Jibber Jabber" disclaimer line. — [Riot legal](https://www.riotgames.com/en/legal)
- The LCK creator guideline (2022) allows LCK IP only for personal, non-commercial use (platform income such as ad revenue, donations and subscriptions on free content is allowed) and forbids covering, removing or altering sponsor logos. — [lolesports.com](https://lolesports.com/ko-KR/news/lck-content-creator-guideline)
- CHZZK's guide forbids altering its logo and commercial use. — [CHZZK brand guides](https://chzzk.gitbook.io/chzzk/resources/brand-guides)
- KLeague fonts are free for non-commercial use only and barred from logo/BI use. — [Sandoll Cloud](https://www.sandollcloud.com/free-font/17860/KLeagueDynamic)
- LCK palette values in the repo (`#1C192B` dark, `#E2E4F2` pale, `#FBC3AE` peach, LCK Blue `#6454ED`, LCK Red `#F0383B`) are Namu Wiki values, not official. — [Namu Wiki LCK](https://namu.wiki/w/League%20of%20Legends%20Champions%20Korea)

### Inferences
- The plate's current look (Hextech black `#010A13`, parchment `#F0E6D2`, Hextech gold `#C89B3C`) is LoL-client colour, i.e. Riot's game brand, not the LCK's league brand. It reads "League of Legends", which the repo's own research warned would "look like a Riot product". For an LCK layer that feels like the LCK rather than the game client, move toward the LCK's own family: a violet-cast near-black, a cool pale, a violet-blue and a warm red for sides, with gold dropped or kept only for Flol's own wordmark. The LEC layer would then carry the LEC's palette. This is a call for Craig, not settled.
- Suggested brand-layer token list (LCK values from this file and the repo): ground and paper; text and mid; one accent; side colours (blue side, red side); rise and fall (red/blue in LCK); display face, body face, tracking; motif (one, structural: obangsaek role colours or a dancheong band at edges, per the repo's earlier advice); title language pair (English + Hangul for LCK; English + nothing, or local language, for LEC); voice words (CHZZK-style game slang).
- What makes it Korean rather than Western-with-Korean-words, in one list: (1) Hangul set in a real Korean UI gothic at matched weight with the Latin, tightly tracked; (2) Hangul used as a design element at contrasting scale, not as a translation footnote; (3) red up, blue down; (4) team colours as solid fields; (5) dense stats tables with single-action transaction screens; (6) game slang and 응원/승부예측-style social surfaces; (7) LCK vocabulary (series, POG, 감독). The Western-with-Korean-words failure is: Western display face, Hangul in a fallback font, green-up arrows, gold-fantasy ornament, and taegeuk or hanbok clichés.

### Gaps
- Riot's current terms for third-party fantasy products remain unfound (also a gap in the repo).
- Whether DesignStudio's LCK guidelines define a Hangul typeface: not found.
