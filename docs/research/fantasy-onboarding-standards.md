# Let newcomers pick before they sign up

A free LCK fantasy game should let anyone build a full squad without an account, and ask for one only at the moment there is something to lose: saving the squad before the deadline, or joining a friend's league. The evidence behind this is consistent but mostly indirect. Duolingo reports about a **20% rise in daily active users from moving sign-up back a few steps**, NN/g calls login walls one of the things users are "more annoyed by … than almost anything else", and e-commerce studies attribute **roughly a fifth to a quarter of checkout abandonment to forced account creation**. No published A/B test compares squad-first with account-first in fantasy sports. The user's split holds partly. Riot's own LTA Fantasy does put a Riot account in front of everything. FPL's official guide is account-first as well, so football fantasy is not uniformly "try first". UCL Fantasy's pick-first flow is taken here as the user observed it first-hand; no source this session could reach described UEFA's login timing either way. The rest of the standard follows from the same principle. Pre-fill a valid squad the user edits, and teach each rule at the step where it applies rather than in a tutorial. Offer email codes, Google, Discord and Apple at the claim step, and add passkeys and Riot Sign On later. Keep marketing consent separate and unticked, and treat age as a legal question. Carry first-week reminders by email and a calendar feed, because iPhone web push reaches only home-screen installs. **One limit applies to everything below: the session's network blocked most primary sources (uefa.com, premierleague.com, nngroup.com, baymard.com, fidoalliance.org, webkit.org, developer.riotgames.com, fantasyfootballscout.co.uk) and all of Reddit, so the findings rest on search-result snippets of the cited pages, not full reads.** Nothing was tested hands-on in a logged-out browser.

## Sign-up walls cost users; the hard numbers come from outside games

The best-known figure comes from Duolingo. Its growth lead said that letting people "experience Duolingo without signing up — do a lesson, see the set of skills" raised sign-ups, and that **"simply moving the sign-up screen back a few steps led to about a 20% increase in DAUs"** ([Taplytics](https://taplytics.com/blog/duolingo-ab-test-onboarding)). It is a secondhand quote with no sample size or time window, and a widely shared "Duolingo delayed sign-up" case on DataInterview is explicitly simulated data, so it should not be cited as Duolingo's ([DataInterview](https://www.datainterview.com/ab-testing-lab/delayed-signup)). Duolingo now asks for an account after the first lesson, framed as "save your progress" ([UserGuiding](https://userguiding.com/blog/duolingo-onboarding-ux)). That framing is exactly the moment an LCK game has when a newcomer finishes a squad.

The usability case is older and qualitative. NN/g's "Login Walls Stop Users in Their Tracks" argues that a wall reverses reciprocity by asking for data before giving value. It holds that a wall is justified only where users obviously gain from an account, as with email or banking, and that occasional-use sites suffer most ([NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/login-walls/)). Luke Wroblewski's "gradual engagement" makes the same point from the design side. People should use the product and build up investment before registering. Geni let visitors start a family tree with no sign-up and reported **5 million profiles in 5 months** ([A List Apart](https://alistapart.com/article/signupforms)). He also warns that splitting a form across several pages is not gradual engagement. The strongest hard numbers are from checkout research, where **19% to 26% of abandoners cite forced account creation**, depending on year and base ([Top Growth Marketing](https://topgrowthmarketing.com/dtc-ecommerce-benchmarks/checkout-abandonment-rate/); [eDesk](https://www.edesk.com/blog/shopping-cart-abandonment)). Those figures are attributed to Baymard, whose site could not be reached, and they come from shoppers who already intend to buy. For a game they show direction, not size.

The steelman for an early wall is real. An account captures an email for reminders, and an unsaved squad that vanishes before the deadline is a lost manager. Duolingo's commentators name that trade-off ([UserGuiding](https://userguiding.com/blog/duolingo-onboarding-ux)). The answer is not to drop the ask but to time it. The ask should come when the user has a squad worth saving and a deadline that makes saving urgent. A fantasy game's activation event is concrete: a saved squad that is still there when the first points land. Time to that squad should be measured in minutes. SaaS benchmarks of **37.5% activation and about 1.5 days to value** are the wrong yardstick for it ([Userpilot](https://userpilot.com/blog/one-metric-matters-omtm)). A CleverTap case study on Dream11 found that users who played the previous match were more likely to return for the next ([CleverTap](https://clevertap.com/case-study/fantasy-sports-dream11-retains-5x-customers/)). That points the effort at getting a first squad locked before the first deadline, more than at polishing the sign-up form.

## Fantasy games mostly gate early; the good ones pre-fill the squad

The games themselves fall into three groups, and fewer of them follow "try first" than the user's contrast suggests. **Official LoL fantasy is a hard wall.** LTA Fantasy's FAQ says "you'll need a Riot Account and a Riot ID", and the flow runs sign in, then name, logo and tricode, then a $50 budget, then picks ([Riot support](https://support.riotgames.com/en-us/league-of-legends/events/lta-fantasy-faq); [Esports Insider](https://esportsinsider.com/2025/04/lta-fantasy-sign-up-rules)). Riot has reasons a third party lacks: the identity carries region eligibility (China, Italy, Greece and Vietnam are excluded) and the Riot ID shown on leaderboards. Valorant Pick'Em sidesteps the question by living in the game client, where the player is already signed in ([esports.gg](https://esports.gg/guides/valorant/valorant-pick-ems)).

**FPL is officially account-first too.** Its "how to register" guide runs Register, then MyPL email, then password, name, date of birth and country, then an email code. Only after that does it say "this will take you into the game, where you pick your squad" ([Premier League](https://www.premierleague.com/en/news/2173986)). The live 2026/27 site may differ, but nothing reachable said so. UEFA's own pages list fantasy as a reason to log in. They also note that a login-system update logged everyone out, and a review reports "numerous reports" of login failures in UEFA's gaming app ([UEFA login FAQs](https://www.uefa.com/uefachampionsleague/news/029c-1e749dbf27cd-8991246c028a-1000--login-faqs); [Softonic](https://uefa-champions-league-gaming-hub.en.softonic.com/android)). Login is evidently a known source of friction there. FIFA's World Cup 2026 game says you need a FIFA account "to register and submit your team". The wording fits a build-then-submit flow without proving it ([FFScout](https://www.fantasyfootballscout.co.uk/2026/05/26/how-to-play-the-fifa-world-cup-2026-fantasy-game)). Both UEFA and FIFA treat "save" as a distinct action you must take before the deadline ([UEFA rules](https://www.uefa.com/uefachampionsleague/news/025f-0fd4b42cc0a7-74498b7df63b-1000/)). That makes "save" the natural place for an auth gate.

**The squad builder is where FPL leads.** For 2026/27, FPL offers help at squad selection. The user answers three questions (club preference, bench strength, budget allocation) and FPL generates a squad. Auto Pick now avoids likely non-starters, can be re-rolled, and can refill only the slots the user cleared. It is framed as a "starting point", with unlimited changes before the first deadline ([Premier League](https://www.premierleague.com/en/news/4679873/all-you-need-to-know-about-changes-to-fpl-for-202627); [OneFPL](https://onefpl.com/blog/fpl-assisted-squad-builder-auto-pick-2026-27)). The official wording offers this to *returning* managers on the website, so whether brand-new managers see it is unconfirmed. One World Cup 2026 guide says that squad starts in a default 4-4-2 ([FFScout](https://www.fantasyfootballscout.co.uk/2026/06/11/the-idiots-guide-to-fantasy-fifa-world-cup-2026)). LTA Fantasy has the best first-league pattern. **Picking a favourite team auto-enters you into the LTA, conference and team leaderboards** ([Sheep Esports](https://www.sheepesports.com/articles/lol-a-guide-to-lta-fantasy/en)), which maps directly onto the team supporter leagues already decided for the LCK game. Third-party FPL planners that need no login show there is demand to build without committing ([scoutingstats](https://scoutingstats.ai/fpl/planner)).

**Invites are link-then-account everywhere this research looked.** Sleeper gives each league a unique link. A new user creates an account and is then prompted to join. The link is hidden when the league is full or when the commissioner restricts invites, and Sleeper's fallback for a broken link is "open it in a desktop browser" ([Sleeper](https://support.sleeper.com/en/articles/4645595-inviting-friends-and-finding-public-leagues); [Sleeper](https://support.sleeper.com/en/articles/4240914-my-invite-link-isn-t-working)). That fallback shows how fragile deep links are even for a market leader. World Cup 2026 mini-leagues join by code or link, and FPL lets you set up a mini-league "whether you have already registered a team or not" ([FFScout](https://www.fantasyfootballscout.co.uk/2026/05/26/how-to-play-the-fifa-world-cup-2026-fantasy-game); [Premier League](https://www.premierleague.com/news/2978000)). **No game was found that lets an invitee build a squad before having an account and then joins them to the league on save.** That gap is an opening for the LCK game.

**Late joiners score from when they join, and catch-up comes from fresh-start competitions.** FPL has no registration deadline, but late joiners miss earlier points. Its classic leagues can start scoring from a chosen gameweek ([Premier League](https://www.premierleague.com/news/2978000)). F1's mid-season Legends League starts everyone level ([Formula1.com](https://formula1.com/en/latest/article/the-legends-league-a-second-chance-to-play-f1-fantasy-this-season.6pSkDlsiETmx0C1uxeZygw)). BLAST's esports fantasy counts only matches that begin after you lock in, so that late entrants cannot exploit known results ([BLAST rulebook](https://blast.tv/cs/news/fantasy/rulebook)). For a split season the per-split table is the natural late-joiner answer. Split 2 then becomes a real second start, matching the decided chip reset.

## Teach by doing, with a different first lesson for each audience

The one quantitative study on tutorials argues against front-loading them. In NN/g's test of 70 users across four apps, people who read deck-of-cards tutorials reached **91% task success against 94% for those who skipped**. Their task times were no better (93.5 s against 85.2 s), and they rated the tasks harder (p = 0.047) ([NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/mobile-tutorials/)). The study compared tutorial against no tutorial, not against contextual help, and the pages championing contextual help are vendors' own ([Cobe](https://www.cobeisfresh.com/blog/why-contextual-help-outperforms-onboardings-in-ux-design)). Game UX points the same way. Celia Hodent's GDC talk on onboarding puts "active tutorials" under learning ([Class Central](https://classcentral.com/course/youtube-the-gamer-s-brain-part-2-ux-of-onboarding-and-player-engagement-158058)). Riot's own 2018 new-player post aimed not to bury players in facts and features before they decide whether to invest ([Riot Nexus](https://nexus.leagueoflegends.com/en-au/2018/05/dev-new-player-experience)). NN/g's empty-state guidance completes the picture. An empty screen should say why it is empty and offer the next action ([NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/videos/empty-states-in-application-design-guidelines/)). In practice this means a pre-filled, valid squad the user edits, with each rule appearing the first time it bites: budget when a pick overspends, the per-team limit when it is hit, the captain when the save button asks for one.

The two audiences need different first lessons. **LoL fans new to fantasy** know the players and roles but not the economy. For them, budget, the weekly deadline, captaincy, the bench and transfer hits are the new ideas, and each should be explained in one line at the step where it applies. **FPL players new to the LCK** know the economy cold but not the league. For them, the player card does the teaching: role, team, one line of story, recent form and what earns points in LoL. A third-party beginner guide gives the right instinct for newcomers to the league. It describes the LCK as historically the most successful region internationally and advises picking one team or player to connect with first ([Golden Road](https://goldenroadlol.com/guides/lol-esports-beginners-guide/)). That is a direct argument for a "who do you support?" opening question. Formula 1's experience with Drive to Survive shows personality stories pulling in casual fans, but with fading force. The show was a "major reason" for **22% of fans in 2023, down from 30%** about 18 months earlier, and social media (22%) and family (21%) now outrank it as ways in ([Morning Consult](https://pro.morningconsult.com/analysis/drive-to-survive-f1-popularity-new-fans); [The Nightly](https://thenightly.com.au/sport/the-real-reason-why-formula-ones-fanbase-is-shifting-and-its-not-the-netflix-effect-of-drive-to-survive-c-14521819)). One-line player stories help, but a friend's invite is the stronger door, which is why the invite path below gets its own flow.

The official LoL esports side offers almost nothing to induct new fans. Explainers of the draft phase, lanes and objectives come from media outlets ([Dot Esports](https://dotesports.com/league-of-legends/news/beginners-guide-to-watching-the-lcs-and-lec)), and English VODs come from LCK Global's channels ([Golden Road](https://goldenroadlol.com/guides/lol-esports-beginners-guide/)). A short "how to watch the LCK" link from the squad screen fills a real gap. In US draft products the practice space is the mock draft ([ESPN](https://support.espn.com/hc/en-us/articles/360000139532-Mock-Draft)), but this game is not a draft game. Its equivalent is unlimited free edits before the first deadline, which FPL and UCL already allow ([UEFA](https://www.uefa.com/uefachampionsleague/news/02a8-2170e4d64843-0abeb4a65b12-1000--champions-league-fantasy-football-picking-your-squad/)).

## Sign in with a code, a social button or a passkey; claim the guest squad carefully

**Email one-time codes beat magic links for this audience.** Magic links fail in documented ways. They open in the mail app's in-app browser, so the user is signed in there and not in their real browser. Some providers bind the link to the device that requested it. Email security scanners can consume one-time links before the user clicks ([Hanko](https://www.hanko.io/blog/magic-links-vs-email-passcodes); [Auth0](https://support.auth0.com/center/s/article/passwordless-magic-link-error-The-link-must-be-opened-on-the-same-device-and-browser-from-which-you-submitted-your-email-address)). A guest claiming a squad is exactly the case where the wrong browser loses the squad. A typed code keeps the claim in the tab that holds it.

**Social login has the best evidence on sign-up conversion, although it comes from vendors.** Google's case studies report about **2× conversion for Reddit and +47% web sign-ups for Pinterest**, with no published method ([Google](https://developers.google.com/identity/sign-in/case-studies/reddit?authuser=5); [Google](https://developers.google.com/identity/sign-in/case-studies/pinterest?authuser=0)). Discord's OAuth is self-serve with the `identify` and `email` scopes ([Discord](https://docs.discord.com/developers/topics/oauth2)), and it is where LoL communities already gather. Third-party trackers put its users at roughly 200 million a month and survey 36% of gamers as daily or weekly users ([DemandSage](https://www.demandsage.com/?p=13874)), but no data compares Discord with Google login for esports fans. Apple's rule that iOS apps with social login must also offer a privacy-preserving option such as Sign in with Apple does not bind a web-only product ([Apple Developer Forums](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/765145)). Offering Apple is a courtesy to iPhone users, not an obligation.

**Passkeys are the best credential but a poor first ask.** The FIDO Alliance's 2026 index reports **93% sign-in success for passkeys against 78% for passwords with MFA, and 36% of successful sign-ins now using a passkey** ([FIDO Passkey Index 2026](https://fidoalliance.org/passkey-index-2026/)). That index draws on a small group of large members, not a random sample. A passkey needs an account to attach to, so for a guest it comes second. The natural moment is after the claim: "sign in faster next time". **Riot Sign On is not a launch option.** It requires an approved production API key, and Riot wants a working public site with terms and a privacy policy, not a repo ([Riot developer FAQ](https://developer.riotgames.com/docs/faqs)). Clients are issued only after approval ([Riot developer docs](https://developer.riotgames.com/docs/valorant)). Riot's policy also requires that players opt in, with a disclaimer that linking makes their data public ([Riot Developer API Policy](https://support-developer.riotgames.com/hc/en-us/articles/22698735834515)). RSO only identifies a player's own account and supplies no pro-match data. Its value is profile flair and trust, so it fits as an optional "link your Riot account" once the site is live.

**The guest-then-claim pattern is well established, and its failure modes are known.** Firebase's anonymous auth shows the shape. The app creates an anonymous user, then links a real credential so the data carries over. It also shows the collision: the `credential-already-in-use` error when the chosen Google or Discord account already owns a team ([Firebase](https://firebase.google.com/docs/auth/flutter/account-linking)). The game needs an explicit rule for that case. With one team per account per season, the simplest rule is to show both squads side by side and ask which to keep, never merging them silently. The other traps are the browser's own. A guest squad held only in local storage disappears in a private window, on another device, or when site data is cleared. The squad should therefore live server-side under an anonymous session from the first edit, with the claim prompt coming before the deadline, not after it.

## Consent, age and accessibility are build requirements, not polish

**Consent should be narrow and unbundled.** The ICO's guidance says consent must be separate from the terms, granular and not a precondition of service. Pre-ticked boxes and silence are not consent, each e-marketing channel needs its own unticked opt-in, and withdrawing must be as easy as giving ([ICO](https://cy.ico.org.uk/for-organisations/uk-gdpr-guidance-and-resources/lawful-basis/consent/what-is-valid-consent)). Running an account a user asked for is better grounded in contract than in consent. The claim screen should therefore carry one plain line about terms and privacy, plus one unticked box for news. Deadline reminders the user switches on are a feature setting, not marketing.

**Age is the riskiest part for a UK hobby developer, and it needs legal advice, not just UX.** The UK's digital age of consent is 13, and GDPR's default is 16, which member states may lower to 13 ([Lewis Silkin](https://www.lewissilkin.com/en/insights/2026/04/17/age-assurance-in-2026-what-do-digital-businesses-operating-in-the-uk-and-eu-need-to-know)). The UK Children's Code covers games likely to be used by under-18s ([Wiggin](https://www.wiggin.co.uk/app/uploads/2022/05/The-Childrens-Code-–-A-quick-practical-overview-for-games-businesses.pdf)). A LoL esports game plainly attracts teenagers. The ICO's 2026 position hardened. Its March open letter says services with a minimum age of 13 that rely on bare self-declaration generally lack a lawful basis for under-13s' data ([ICO](https://cy.ico.org.uk/about-the-ico/open-letter-to-social-media-and-video-sharing-platforms-on-strengthening-age-assurance/)), and its £14.47m fine against Reddit cited self-declaration as insufficient where children face risk ([Osborne Clarke](https://www.osborneclarke.com/insights/uk-ico-fines-online-platform-ps1447m-and-warns-age-self-declaration-not-enough-protect)). Those cases concern social platforms. A fantasy game without chat, public profiles, cash or ads is low-risk by comparison, and the Children's Code accepts self-declaration for low-risk services ([NI Business Info](https://www.nibusinessinfo.co.uk/content/comply-childrens-code-protect-childrens-privacy-online)). The defensible design is low-data by default: a neutral date-of-birth question at the claim step, no free-text chat, private profiles, and team names moderated by a word filter. Country-by-country EU thresholds were not verified this session.

**Two WCAG 2.2 criteria land directly on sign-up.** 3.3.8 Accessible Authentication (Minimum, AA) bars cognitive tests such as recalling or transcribing a password, or solving a puzzle CAPTCHA, unless an alternative exists. Passkeys, social login and paste-friendly fields comply, while copying a code from a phone to a desktop counts as a transcription task ([GOV.UK guide to 3.3.8](https://alphagov.github.io/guide-to-wcag/sc/3.3.8.html)). The email code field must therefore accept paste and one-time-code autofill. 3.3.7 Redundant Entry (A) bars asking again for data already given in the same process ([GOV.UK guide to 3.3.7](https://alphagov.github.io/guide-to-wcag/sc/3.3.7.html)). The team name and favourite team chosen as a guest must carry into the claim form. Four other 2.2 criteria matter for a squad builder, from the standard itself rather than this session's searches ([W3C WCAG 2.2](https://www.w3.org/TR/WCAG22/)). Under 2.5.7 Dragging Movements, a drag-to-bench pitch needs a tap alternative. Under 2.5.8 Target Size, targets must be at least 24 × 24 CSS px. Under 2.4.11 Focus Not Obscured, a sticky "save" bar or cookie banner must not cover the focused control. Under 3.2.6 Consistent Help, help must sit in the same place on every screen.

## The first week runs on email and a calendar feed, with push as a bonus

**The permission ask belongs after the first save, never on arrival.** Chrome brought in a quieter permission UI that switches on automatically for sites with very low accept rates, and asking on first arrival is how sites earn those rates ([Chromium blog](https://blog.chromium.org/2020/01/introducing-quieter-permission-ui-for.html)). Firefox and Safari require a user gesture before a permission request ([Chrome developers](https://developer.chrome.com/en/blog/permissions-chip/)). A user who blocks the native prompt cannot be asked again, so the advice is a soft in-page prompt first ([OneSignal](https://onesignal.com/blog/boost-your-web-push-opt-in-rates/)). **On iPhone, web push works only for web apps added to the Home Screen (iOS 16.4 and later); an ordinary Safari tab cannot ask** ([WebKit](https://webkit.org/blog/13878/web-push-for-web-apps-on-ios-and-ipados/); [9to5Mac](https://9to5mac.com/2023/02/16/iphone-web-app-new-features-ios-16-4/)). For most iPhone users of a web-first game, reminders therefore have to come by email and calendar.

**A per-user calendar feed fills a real gap.** No official LoL esports calendar sync turned up, only community feeds, a browser extension and an ICS feed published in UTC that leaves users to convert ([Smart Calendars](https://smartcalendars.ai/en/feeds/league-of-legends-schedule); [zlypher/lol-events](https://github.com/zlypher/lol-events)). The deadline matters more here than in FPL. The earlier weekly-logic research proposed a single lock at **Wednesday 06:30 UTC (15:30 KST)**, which falls on Tuesday evening on the US west coast ([Goal](https://www.goal.com/en-gb/news/when-next-fpl-deadline-dates-times-premier-league-fantasy-team/blt3ada2bac6c9821f9) for FPL's 90-minute rule; series start at 08:00 UTC per [esports-news.co.uk](https://esports-news.co.uk/?p=174580)). A feed with each week's deadline and the user's players' series, plus an email the day before the lock, does more than any streak badge. Google Calendar refreshes subscribed feeds slowly, so a moved deadline must also go out by email.

**The habit unit is the gameweek, not the day.** Duolingo reports big retention jumps at the second and seventh day of a streak, and it lets users hold up to two Streak Freezes ([Duolingo blog](https://blog.duolingo.com/how-duolingo-streak-builds-habit)). In fantasy, the equivalent is "set your squad N weeks running", and the freeze already exists, because the last saved squad carries over. This mapping is inference. The game should make the carry-over visible, with a "your squad from last week will play; no changes needed" line, rather than inventing daily tasks. Vendor push benchmarks (Airship's iOS median near 49%) are for native apps and do not transfer to web push ([Airship](https://www.airship.com/mobile-app-push-notification-benchmarks-for-2026/)).

## Localisation: in-game names on screen, stable IDs underneath, local time everywhere

LCK players are known worldwide by their Latin-script in-game names. Faker is Lee Sang-hyeok (이상혁) in Korean records and "Faker" everywhere fans look ([ONE Esports](https://www.oneesports.gg/culture/k-pop-idol-daniel-kang-plays-league-of-legends-with-teddy-and-faker/)). In-game names change, as Ruler's did ([Inven Global](https://www.invenglobal.com/datalab/articles/2410/video-interview-ssg-ruler-on-his-new-nickname-playing-better-and-cooling-himself-off-in-booth)). The squad, leagues, history and achievements must therefore key on a stable player ID, never the display name. This is the same rule the platform already applies to FPL's `code`. The player card should lead with the in-game name, team and role, and show the legal name in romanised form and Hangul as secondary detail. No Riot or LCK romanisation guide was found, so use the spelling the player or team uses.

Time follows the decision already made: store in UTC and show in the viewer's local time, with KST on hover or as a second line. Korea has no daylight saving, so a deadline fixed in UTC never moves in Korea, but it shifts by an hour in the UK and the US when their clocks change. The onboarding screen should show the viewer's local deadline time and a countdown. Won-denominated prices, the decided Korean flavour, need a one-line gloss (for example, "₩ is the budget unit, and 억 is 100 million won"; the budget itself is not yet set) the first time they appear. English broadcasts depend on translators to explain Korean context ([Inven Global](https://invenglobal.com/articles/5742/bringing-together-lck-fans-from-across-the-globe-interview-with-spotvs-announcer-and-translator)), and the game's Korean terms and memes in chip and achievement names deserve the same one-line explanation.

## Proposed first-run flow for the LCK game (inference)

Everything in this section is inference from the evidence above, not a pattern observed in a named game. It assumes the squad shape, budget and deadline still to be settled, and it uses the chip and league decisions as they stand.

**Cold arrival, from the landing page to a saved squad and a first league:**

1. **Landing page.** One sentence ("FPL for the LCK: pick a squad of LCK pros, score from their series, beat your friends. Free."), the next deadline in the viewer's local time with a countdown, and one primary button, "Pick your team". No sign-up form, no cookie wall beyond what the law needs, no tour. A smaller "Sign in" link serves returning users.
2. **One optional question.** "Who do you support?" shows the ten LCK team logos plus "No team yet". The answer seeds the auto-pick and later enters the user into that team's supporter league, as LTA Fantasy does. A second, skippable chip row ("I know LoL", "I know FPL", "Both", "New to both") chooses which inline hints appear. No more questions than that.
3. **A full, valid squad already on screen.** The builder opens pre-filled by auto-pick, weighted towards the chosen team, with the budget bar showing what is left. Buttons offer "Re-pick all" and "Fill empty slots" (FPL's 2026/27 pattern). Tapping a player opens a card with role, team, one-line story, recent points and price. Hints appear once, when the user first triggers them: overspending explains the budget, a pick over any per-team limit explains that limit, and the flex and coach slots each get a one-line note. Everything works by tap; drag is optional.
4. **Captain and bench.** Before saving, the builder asks for a captain ("scores double this week") and a vice-captain, then shows the bench order with one line on auto-subs.
5. **Team name.** A suggested, editable name. This is the last thing the guest types.
6. **Save, then claim.** "Save team" opens a sheet: "Save your team so it plays from Wednesday 15:30 KST (Tue 23:30 in Los Angeles)". Options are Continue with Google, Continue with Discord, Continue with Apple, or Email me a code. Below them sit a date-of-birth field, one line linking terms and privacy, and one unticked "Send me game news" box. The squad, team name and favourite team carry over untouched (WCAG 3.3.7). If the credential already owns a team, the sheet shows both squads and asks which to keep.
7. **Confirmation that shows the league.** "You're in" lists the leagues joined automatically, the global league and the supporter league, each with the user's rank shown as "starts Week 1". Two buttons follow: "Create a league for friends", which produces a share link, and "Join with a code".
8. **Reminders, offered once.** Default options are "Email me the day before each deadline" and "Add deadlines and my players' matches to my calendar" (an ICS link). Web push appears only where the browser supports it, and on iPhone only after "Add to Home Screen". "Sign in faster next time" offers a passkey.
9. **First week.** The game sends one email the day before the lock and one after the week's points are confirmed ("you scored 42, 3rd of 8 in Pub League"). Once the lock passes, the squad page shows "Your squad is locked. Points update after each series."

**Arriving from a friend's invite link (`/join/ABC123`):**

1. **Invite landing.** "Sam invited you to Pub League: 7 managers, classic scoring, points from Week 3." The primary button reads "Pick your team and join", with "Already playing? Sign in to join" beneath it. The invite code goes into the URL and the anonymous server session at once, so it survives the sign-in redirect and an in-app browser.
2. **Same builder, steps 2 to 5 above,** with the league's name pinned at the top of the screen as the reason to finish.
3. **Claim.** The save sheet reads "Save your team and join Pub League". Discord is listed first, because invite links usually travel through Discord, and the email code still works when the link was opened in Discord's in-app browser.
4. **Land in the league table, not the squad page.** The user's row is highlighted next to Sam's, and the global and supporter leagues are listed beneath.
5. **Edge cases, stated plainly.** If the league is full, or the commissioner has turned off invites, the page says so and offers the global league. A head-to-head league whose fixtures are already set cannot be joined, so it offers that league's classic table or the next split's. Mid-split, it says "Your points count from Week N". If the visitor already has a team, they join with that team and no guest squad is created.

**Late joiners on either path** see the current gameweek's deadline and "Points count from this week". The split table and per-split leagues are offered as a fresh start, and all transfers are free until their first deadline.

## Do's and don'ts

| Do | Don't |
|---|---|
| Let anyone build and edit a full squad with no account | Put a sign-up wall, a tour or a rules page before the squad |
| Pre-fill a valid squad from a favourite team, with re-roll and partial fill | Start newcomers with an empty pitch and a full budget |
| Explain each rule in one line the first time it applies | Explain LoL to FPL players and fantasy to LoL fans in the same words |
| Ask for an account at "save" or "join", tied to the deadline | Ask for notification permission on arrival |
| Offer an email code, Google, Discord and Apple; add a passkey after | Rely on magic links alone, or wait for Riot Sign On to launch |
| Keep guest squads server-side and define the account-collision rule | Hold guest squads only in local storage |
| Auto-enter the global and supporter leagues; put the invite at the centre | Let invite context drop during sign-in |
| Send email and a calendar feed for deadlines; offer push where it works | Promise push reminders to iPhone users browsing in Safari |
| Key players on stable IDs; show in-game names and local times | Key data on in-game names, or show KST-only times |
| Use separate, unticked marketing consent and a low-data design for minors | Bundle consent into the terms, or rely on self-declared age without legal advice |

## Conclusion

The user's instinct is right, but the contrast is not "football does it right, LoL does it wrong". Most official games, FPL included, still gate early. The ones that defer the account turn "save" into a deliberate, deadline-bound act, and that act is where an independent LCK game can ask for an account at no cost to the newcomer. The real advantage over Riot's LTA Fantasy is not only removing the wall. A hobby game can join an invitee to a friend's league in the same motion that saves their first squad, which no game in this research was found doing. Because the game has none of Riot's identity requirements, the account can come last.

The weakest links are not the UX choices but the evidence and the law. Every conversion figure here is secondhand or a vendor's own, and no fantasy game has published a squad-first against account-first test. The game should therefore measure its own funnel from the LCK Cup beta: landing, then squad built, then saved, then still playing in week 2. Age assurance for a teen-heavy esports audience is the one area where the right answer depends on a lawyer rather than a designer. A hands-on, logged-out pass through UCL Fantasy, FPL's 2026/27 builder and LTA Fantasy would confirm the flows this report could only read about in snippets.
