## T1

### For someone new

**The logo.** At a glance it looks like a car maker's badge with no frame: just the letters T1, tilted forward as if speeding. It is all one bright, solid red. The top bar of the T stretches left into a long, pointed arm, with three thinner bars beneath it, each shorter than the last, like the feathers of a wing. The 1 has a small flag-like hook at its top. On its world championship kit, the team has worn the same mark in gold, with one star per world title.

**The team.** T1 is South Korea's most famous team in League of Legends, a five-a-side online battle game. It is owned by a joint company of SK Telecom, Korea's biggest mobile network, and Comcast Spectacor, an American sports company. It has won six world championships and more titles in the LCK, Korea's top league, than any other team. Its oldest rivalry, the Telecom War, is with KT, another phone company. Its star is Faker (real name Lee Sang-hyeok), widely called the greatest player ever; fans nickname him the Unkillable Demon King. His teammates go by Doran, Oner, Peyz and Keria. Fans joke that T1 stumble in the Korean league, then come alive at the world championship. They won it three years running, until a semi-final loss in November 2026. Unhappy fans sometimes hire trucks with big screens to park outside the office with messages, now a running joke among fans.

### Logo

A bare red "T1" wordmark (#E2012D in the Wikipedia SVG), with no shield, circle or badge. Both letters lean hard right, about 34 degrees off upright, and their stems run parallel with rounded feet. The T's top bar is lopsided: it has a long left arm ending in a sharp, upswept curved tip and a short right arm. Under the left arm sit three stacked bars, each shorter than the one above, with the same curved left tip and right ends cut flush against the slanted stem, like a wing. The 1 has a curved flag at the top that echoes the T's tip. The mark is about 2.5 to 1. This mark has been in use since October 2019, and nothing shows a change for 2026.

**Meaning.** T1 has published no meaning for the shapes. The 2019 reveal said the wing was kept because it had been the team's symbol for years. The reveal called the new mark cleaner and more modern, and its tagline was "The revolution has already begun". On the gold Worlds version, each star stands for one World Championship.

**Before.** Up to October 2019 (SK Telecom T1 / SKT T1), the logo was a red winged T1 inside a red circle, with SK Telecom wording. On 8 October 2019, before the Worlds 2019 group stage, the team became T1 and dropped the circle and the SKT name, keeping the wing. That bare winged wordmark is still the main logo. Worlds kits carry a gold version with a star for each World title. The 2024 Worlds kit had four stars, and a fifth was reportedly added after the 2024 title. T1 won a sixth title in 2025.

**A fan crest must avoid.** A crest must avoid a wing or speed stripes coming off a letter, especially three stacked bars that get shorter and end flush against a slanted stroke. It must avoid a heavily slanted, italic "T1" or "T", and a T with one long upswept arm. It must avoid a bare wordmark in only that solid red (#E2012D), a red circle around a winged mark (the old SKT logo), and a gold mark with a row of stars.

**Unsure.** I read the shape details straight from the Wikipedia SVG's path data. The 34 degree slant, the short right arm of the T and the stripes ending flush against the stem all come from that geometry, not from T1. T1 has never published #E2012D as an official brand colour, and the colour comes only from that SVG. Two claims come only from search summaries, since Dot Esports, esports.gg and Liquipedia/Fandom would not load (403, 402 or DNS errors): that a fifth star was added after the 2024 title, and that the gold star logo is kept for Worlds kits and left off LCK kits. Whether a sixth star appeared after the 2025 title is unconfirmed. The old SKT logo, a red winged T1 in a red circle, is confirmed only by search summaries, because Dexerto does not describe it. I found no source on the 2026 LCK kit's logo. Still, nothing points to a change since 2019, and Wikipedia records none. An LBB Online "T1 new brand identity" story is about an unrelated Toronto agency of the same name.

## Gen.G

### For someone new

**The logo.** At a glance it looks like a gold shield, or a heart, cut straight down the middle. The whole thing is one flat, pale gold, with no words on it. Each half is a chunky, angular letter G. The right one is a mirror image of the left, so the two face each other. Their tops are flat and their bottoms slope inward to a point, which gives the heart shape. The narrow gap between them is shaped like an upright sword.

**The team.** Gen.G is a South Korean esports team owned by investors led by Kevin Chou, co-founder of the mobile games company Kabam. It began in 2017 by buying Samsung Galaxy, that year's world champions. Its two Gs also spell GG, gamer shorthand for good game. It calls itself the tigers and its fans TigerNation. In 2026 it won both titles in Korea's league, the LCK, beating Hanwha Life 3-1 in the September final. Its great rival is T1, Korea's most famous team. Its best-known players are Chovy, who fans joke collects in-game gold faster than anyone, and Ruler, nicknamed the Last Galaxy because he is the last player left from the Samsung team. For years fans joked that it ruled Korea but fell in the world championship semi-finals. In 2026 it finally reached the final and lost 3-1 to China's Bilibili Gaming.

Sources: <https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gen.G>, <https://pulse2.com/gen-g-46-million/>, <https://www.invenglobal.com/articles/20275/chovy-says-roster-stability-key-to-2026-lck-cup-win>, <https://www.esports.net/news/gaming/bilibili-gaming-beat-gen-g-3-1-to-win-worlds-2026-after-knocking-out-all-four-lck-teams/>, <https://insider-gaming.com/blg-wins-lol-worlds-2026-and-first-summoners-cup/>

### Logo

A shield made of two separate halves with a narrow vertical gap between them. Each half is a blocky, angular "G". The right G mirrors the left, so the two face each other. Together they make a wide shield with a flat top and straight sides. Lower down, both halves angle inward towards one point at the bottom, so the outline looks like a heart or a crest. The gap runs the full height and never closes. Near the top, the open mouths of the two Gs line up into a crossbar. Gap and crossbar together leave an upright sword or cross in negative space. For 2026 both halves are the same soft pale gold, which I sampled at about #D2B984. There is no text and the corners are slightly rounded.

**Meaning.** The 2023 brand guidelines quote co-founder Kevin Chou, and Dotesports reported the same in 2018. The two Gs facing each other make a shield, which stands for tank players. The sword in the middle stands for damage dealers (DPS). The heart-shaped outline stands for support or healer players. The two letters also read as GG, short for "good game". The New York agency Emeht made the branding. Gen.G stands for Generation Gaming, a generation redefining what gaming can be. Separately, Gen.G calls itself the tigers of the LCK and uses a "NEW GOLD" identity, and its merchandise uses gold, tiger stripes and dancheong patterns, as on the 2026 Musinsa Worlds patch. The tiger is not part of the logo.

**Before.** KSV eSports, 2017 to May 2018: a black KSV mark (Liquipedia KSV_Black_2018). From 3 May 2018 to about 2022: renamed Gen.G (Generation Gaming), with the two-G shield in gold on the left and black on the right, and a "GEN.G ESPORTS" wordmark in the full lockup. About 2022 to 2025: the same split shield in gold and black. The fan wiki and the brand guide give the gold as #AA8A00, and I sampled about #AA9950 from Liquipedia's 2025 file. 2026 onwards: the same shape, with both halves in one paler gold.

**A fan crest must avoid.** Two mirrored angular G or hook shapes facing each other. A shield split down the middle by a full-height vertical gap. A sword or cross formed in negative space between two halves. A flat-topped shield that narrows to a point and reads as a heart. A half-gold, half-black split. A single flat, pale metallic gold on a geometric shield. A gold tiger is fine only if it is not inside a split shield.

**Unsure.** I found no official 2026 announcement. The all-gold 2026 version rests on Liquipedia's current file, Gen.G_Esports_2026_allmode.png, which I viewed and confirmed is one gold throughout, and on thewiki.kr, a fan-edited wiki. Thewiki.kr says the change came in 2026 and that fans were split on it. The switch year of about 2022 between the 2018 and 2022 versions comes only from thewiki.kr. Shacknews confirms the 3 May 2018 rebrand but does not describe the logo. The meaning and the Emeht credit come from search excerpts of the brand guide PDF and Dotesports; I could not open either page in full. The hex values are my own samples from Liquipedia's PNGs, apart from #AA8A00, which the wiki and the brand guide give.

## Hanwha Life Esports

### For someone new

**The logo.** At a glance it looks like a bird's wing, or three brushstrokes sweeping up to the right. It is three thick curved bands stacked one above another. The top band is the longest and is bright orange. Below it sit two shorter bands, one near-black and one light grey. Each band narrows to a sharp, angled point. There is no animal, letter or shield. In the full version, the name Hanwha Life Esports sits underneath in rounded, dark, slightly computer-style lettering.

**The team.** Hanwha Life Esports plays League of Legends, a team video game, in the LCK, Korea's top league. It is backed by Hanwha Life, an insurance company in the Hanwha group, one of Korea's biggest business groups; its orange is the group's. It joined the league in 2018 and waited six years for a trophy, winning the 2024 summer title. It then won the first LCK Cup in 2025 and the 2026 Mid-Season Invitational, a world event. Its big rival is Gen.G, which beat it 3-1 in the 2026 league final. Its best-known players are Zeus and Gumayusi, who both won world titles at T1, Korea's most famous team. Fans fondly call it a little chick, a joke borrowed from Hanwha's baseball club, whose fans are known for cheering through losing years. In 2020 it was also the Korean G2, after a European team known for surprising character choices.

Sources: <https://www.sheepesports.com/articles/lol-hanwha-life-esports-completed-2026-lck-roster/en>, <https://www.gosugamers.net/lol/news/73013-league-of-legends-hanwha-life-esports-upset-gen-g-to-become-the-lck-summer-champions>, <https://esports.gg/news/league-of-legends/hanwha-life-esports-a-championship-8-years-in-the-making/>, <https://www.strafe.com/news/read/hanwha-life-esports-win-lck-cup-and-become-first-team-at-first-stand/>, <https://www.invenglobal.com/articles/25926/geng-defeats-hanwha-life-esports-to-win-back-to-back-lck-titles>, <https://www.gosugamers.net/lol/news/79163-gen-g-are-the-2026-lck-season-champions-after-defeating-hanwha-life-esports>, <https://www.sheepesports.com/articles/hanwha-life-esports-crowned-msi-2026-champion-after-3-2-against-bilibili-gaming/en>

### Logo

Three thick, tapering arcs stacked into a sweep that rises to the upper right, like a wing. The top arc is the longest and is bright orange (#F3741B). Under it are two shorter arcs: a dark one (black in the Wikimedia SVG, dark grey in descriptions) and a light grey one (#BCBEC1). Each arc has pointed, angled ends. In the full lockup, the name "Hanwha Life Esports" sits below the arcs in a rounded, techno-style dark sans-serif. There is no animal, monogram or shield. HLE did not rebrand for 2026.

**Meaning.** Per the team's April 2018 reveal, the three arches stand for trust, teamwork and innovation, which the team tied to mutual trust, cooperation and creative play. The orange arch sits on top, with the two grey arches below it. The orange echoes the Hanwha group's corporate orange. People often read the shape as a wing, but the team has not said so.

**Before.** 2014 to 2018: HUYA Tigers, GE Tigers, KOO Tigers, then ROX Tigers, each under tiger-era sponsor branding. Those logos were not checked in detail.
April 2018 to now: Hanwha Life bought the ROX Tigers' LCK spot and revealed the three-arch emblem (Inven article, 26 April 2018). It is still in use, and no redesign has been found up to the 2026 season.

**A fan crest must avoid.** Avoid three nested, tapering arcs or swooshes that fan up to the right. Avoid an orange arc stacked over grey or black arcs, and avoid a wing built from curved bands. Avoid an orange, black and grey scheme led by orange near #F3741B with light grey #BCBEC1. Avoid a rounded techno wordmark set under an arc emblem. Keep away from Hanwha corporate orange and from any three-arc layout.

**Unsure.** 1. Hex colours come only from the Wikimedia SVG, which I read: orange #f3741b, light grey #bcbec1, and a middle arc with no fill, so it draws black. No official brand guide was found. Inven calls the two lower arches "different shades of gray", so the middle arc may really be dark grey.
2. 2026 use is inferred, not stated. Wikipedia (flagged as needing an update) shows this logo and records no rebrand. A March 2026 article on renewing the Monster sponsorship and a search for "HLE new logo 2026" also turned up no logo change. No 2026 source names the logo directly.
3. Liquipedia blocked fetching (403), and hle.kr was not read. Namu Wiki and Leaguepedia were not checked.
4. The wing reading is a common interpretation, not the team's own.

## Kiwoom DRX

### For someone new

**The logo.** A chunky X that fills a square, with no letters, a bit like two strips of tape crossed on a parcel. It is dark navy and hot pink on white. One arm of the X is a thick, solid navy bar running from top left to bottom right. The other arm, passing behind it, is made of thin pink stripes with white gaps, like a pinstripe suit. At the top right, a solid pink corner caps that striped arm, so it looks like an arrow pointing up and right.

**The team.** Kiwoom DRX is an independent Korean club in League of Legends, a team video game. Since March 2026 it has carried the name of Kiwoom Securities, an online stock brokerage, hence the logo's navy and pink. Under earlier names it won Korea's league twice, in 2017 and 2018. Its great story came in 2022: it entered the world championship as Korea's fourth-best team, starting in the qualifying round, and beat T1, Korea's most famous team, in the final. No team from that round had ever won. Fans still quote that year's motto, a heart that never breaks. Its star then, Deft, once joked on April Fools' Day that he was secretly an alpaca; he has since left. The 2026 team is built around Ucal and Rich. Games against BNK FEARX are nicknamed the Bank Derby, as both carry finance names. Results lately sit near the bottom, but fans keep believing.

### Logo

The current logo is a square mark with no letters, just a bold, chunky X. One stroke is a thick solid navy bar (about #1E2C5D) that runs from top left to bottom right, with flat-cut ends. It sits in front of the other stroke, which runs from bottom left to top right and is made of thin parallel hot-pink stripes (about #E4007F) with white gaps. At the top right, a solid pink right angle (a top edge and a right edge) frames the striped arm, so it reads as an arrowhead pointing up and right. Navy and pink are the sponsor's colours.

**Meaning.** I found no official explanation of the shape. The X comes from the DragonX name (DRX is short for DragonX). The 2023 rebrand came with the slogans PIONEER, ENJOY CHALLENGE and PAVE THE WAY, but they were not tied to the logo. The team site uses an Unbreakable Spirit tagline and an UNBREAKABLES wordmark. Namu Wiki says the 2026 navy and pink come from the sponsor, Kiwoom Securities. The new arrowhead points up and right, which might hint at a rising market, but no source says so. Fans were mixed on the change: VLR users compared the new colours to Paper Rex, and Namu reports Korean fans disliked them.

**Before.** 2012–2016 Incredible Miracle, then Longzhu Gaming (2016–2018), then Kingzone DragonX (2018–2019); no descriptions of these logos were found. In October 2019 the team became DragonX, later shortened to DRX, and took an X logo. Fans were split, and some said it looked like Alphabet's X logo and a fan-made SpaceX concept; DRX and that designer agreed it was not plagiarism. In 2023 the logo was updated (Wikipedia's file is named DRX_logo_2023). The December 2024 brand kit shows a single royal blue X (about #1003A3): a solid bar from top left to bottom right over a striped arm. It has no arrowhead corner; the stripes simply stop at the square's edge. It also comes boxed (white on blue) and beside an UNBREAKABLES wordmark. In March 2026, Kiwoom Securities signed a naming deal (MoU on 19 March), so the team became Kiwoom DRX, tricode KRX. The X was recoloured navy and pink and gained the pink arrowhead corner at the top right.

**A fan crest must avoid.** Avoid these in a fan-made crest: any large single X or saltire, above all one with one arm solid and the other broken into parallel diagonal stripes or hatching; diagonal pinstripe fills; a right-angle arrowhead corner pointing up and right; and a square, edge-to-edge badge built on an X with no letters. Avoid navy with hot pink as the main colours, and royal blue on its own. Also avoid condensed, heavy capital wordmarks like UNBREAKABLES, and the letters KRX or DRX set as a mark.

**Unsure.** No written source describes the shape; the description comes from the official PNGs: the June 2026 file in navy and pink, and the December 2024 kit in blue. I checked both images again. They confirm that the 2026 version is the current one and that it adds the arrowhead corner, which the 2024 version lacks. The hex values were read from the PNG pixels; they are not from a published brand guide, and I could not re-check them this time because PIL was not available. The tricode KRX comes from Namu Wiki and VLR user posts; Wikipedia says only 'Kiwoom DRX', and press reports give the MoU (19 March) but not the tricode. It is not confirmed whether the 2019 X already had the striped arm. VLR users call the colour dark purple, which does not match the navy in the official file. No descriptions of the Kingzone or Longzhu logos were found. Liquipedia, Leaguepedia and namu.wiki blocked fetching.
