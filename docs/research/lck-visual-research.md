## How to read this

Each value carries one of these marks.

- **Primary**: read from the owner's own page or file today.
- **Wiki**: read from a wiki's page (Namu Wiki, Wikipedia, Leaguepedia). Editors chose it. Good, not official.
- **Press**: a news site's description.
- **Unverified**: from general knowledge or a source I could not open. Check before relying on it.

No hex in this file is my reading of an image. Where no source gave a hex, the cell says so.

## 1. The LCK brand

### The 2021 rebrand

- Launched January 2021 with franchising. It replaced the eagle-and-star badge. (Press: Inven Global, 3 Jan 2021.)
- The logo is two overlapping isosceles triangles. The LCK's own note, as quoted by Inven: "It represents the past and the future, two sides of the rift - and the powerful outstretched wings of a bird in flight." It also "pays respect to the precise angles and star-inspired geometry of our historical mark."
- Motto: "We Make Legends" (전설을 만들어 갑니다). Still on the league's Namu page in 2026.
- Look, as press described it: contrast, minimalism, atmosphere. Bright colours "inspired by the sky". A "boxy" sans serif to match the logo's angles. Stats shown plainly so the players and the game stay the focus. Press compared it to the LEC's 2019 look.
- The 2021 opening title was made by the studio 2grey (Vimeo page, Press).
- The brand site was `brand.lck.co.kr`. It no longer resolves (DNS failure today). The archive copy could not be fetched from here.
- **Agency: not found.** No source names who designed it. **Typeface name: not found.**

### Refreshes since

- No new logo found. The two-triangle mark is still in use in 2026.
- 2026 season line: "LEAVE YOUR MARK" (Wiki: Namu). It is also the title song, by KiiiKiii, with a "pt. 2" for the finals (Press: Soompi, Sheep Esports).
- The LCK company was folded back into Riot Games Korea on 30 Oct 2025 (Wiki: Namu).
- LoL Park is now "CHZZK LoL Park" after Naver's 2026 to 2030 deal. Naver (CHZZK) and SOOP hold the Korean streams. CHZZK-branded space and seats are in the arena (Press: Inven Global, Korean press).

### The 2026 broadcast look

- What changed in 2026 is data, not style: GRID with Naver/CHZZK added a live win-probability graph, a match timeline and an early-game lead read-out (Press: esports.gg, about April 2026).
- No article describing a 2026 overlay redesign was found. Treat the look as the 2021 system, evolved. **Unverified** beyond that.

## 2. LoL's visual language

### Brand

- Riot's 2019 logo refresh was by Pentagram (partner Emily Oberman) for the game's tenth year. Their line: "The new logo still evokes an ancient world of warriors and magic, but looks more timeless and contemporary." The wordmark is drawn from Sharp Sans, made heavier, with small serifs on the L and A. The 3D version is "burnished gold". (Primary: pentagram.com.)
- Riot's old brand site (`brand.riotgames.com`) now redirects to the downloads page. That page offers logo packs (gold, flat gold, black and white), "Hextech" metal and magic assets, and both fonts. It prints no colour values and no usage terms. (Primary.)

### In-game HUD and minimap

- Teams are blue side and red side. Blue's base is bottom-left, red's is top-right. The map is drawn with the river running top-left to bottom-right, three lanes (top, mid, bot) and jungle between.
- A client setting recolours allies blue and enemies red whatever side you start on. A red-green colour-blind mode exists. (Wiki: Riot's own LoL wiki, Settings.)
- **No published hex values for the minimap were found.** The terrain is dark green-brown, the river blue-grey, lanes pale sand. That is description, not a value. If the Rift view wants them, pick our own and say so.

### Role icons

Read from the client's SVG files today (Primary):

- 34 by 34 grid. Flat. Two tones: the lit part in gold `#C8AA6E`, the rest in `#785A28` at 50% opacity.
- **Top, mid and bot are one idea.** A square frame stands for the map. Top lights the top-left corner bracket. Bot lights the bottom-right bracket. Mid lights the diagonal bar. The unlit parts stay faintly visible.
- **Jungle** is a three-pronged leaf or claw shape, all lit.
- **Support** (the file calls it "utility") is a winged staff, all lit.
- Riot also ships "-light" and "-red" variants of each.

These files are Riot's. See the suggestions: draw our own on the same idea.

### Esports broadcast conventions

- Blue side on the left of the scoreboard, red side on the right. Top bar: team tags, kills, gold, towers, dragons, timer in the middle. (Unverified in a written source; it is how every Riot broadcast is laid out.)
- The gold-difference graph: time along the bottom, zero line in the middle, the area above in blue-side colour and below in red-side colour. (Unverified in a written source.)
- Roles are always listed top, jungle, mid, bot, support, top to bottom.
- 2026 added the win-probability graph (Press, above).
- Superstition worth knowing: Worlds winners alternate black and white kits by year. 2026 is "the year of white" (Press: Sheep Esports, 13 Oct 2026).

Things that hold for every shirt:

- LCK rules require the player's game name on the shirt (Wiki: Namu, Dplus KIA uniform page). So "name on the back" is true to life.
- The naming sponsor sits on the chest. Sleeve patches carry the league and other sponsors.
- Most teams wear a jacket over the shirt on stage. T1's is black, Gen.G's Worlds one is white.
- Three reds (T1, KT, NS) and two blues (DNS, KRX) will clash at icon size. Body colour plus trim has to tell them apart, not hue alone.

### Riot's fan policy ("Legal Jibber Jabber", last updated August 2018)

https://www.riotgames.com/en/legal

- Allowed: "We support players who use our intellectual property ("IP") to create free fan projects". The licence is "strictly for noncommercial (except as specifically provided below) community use."
- Not allowed: "You may not create commercial Projects, including any Project that crowdsources any portion of its funding".
- Logos: "you may not use any of our logos or trademarks anywhere in your Project" without a written licence. That covers the LoL logo and, on a plain reading, the LCK logo.
- Other people's rights: "You may not use a third party's IP in your Project without their permission." Team crests, team kit designs and sponsor marks are the teams' and sponsors', not Riot's. Riot's policy cannot grant them.
- Apps: "We prohibit the use of our IP in games and apps." A fantasy game is a game. Read strictly, this cuts against using Riot's art at all, and Riot's API terms are the route it points to instead.
- Required line: "[The title of your Project] was created under Riot Games' "Legal Jibber Jabber" policy using assets owned by Riot Games. Riot Games does not endorse or sponsor this project."
- The catch-all: "we can still shut down any Project at any time if we decide it misinterprets these rules or uses our IP inappropriately."
- The policy says nothing about pro players, teams or fantasy.

### The LCK's own creator guideline (13 June 2022)

https://lolesports.com/ko-KR/news/lck-content-creator-guideline

- "개인적이고 비상업적인 목적과 형태, 방식으로만 LCK IP를 활용한 콘텐츠를 제작하고 이용할 수 있으며": LCK IP may be used only for personal, non-commercial purposes.
- "어떠한 경우에도 LCK 스폰서사의 로고를 가리거나, 제거하거나, 변환해서는 안 됩니다": never cover, remove or alter LCK sponsor logos.
- It is written for video creators. It does not mention player likeness, team logos or fantasy.

### Likeness is separate from copyright

- A photo has two owners' rights in it: the photographer's copyright (Riot or the team) and the player's likeness.
- Korea wrote likeness into law in 2022. The Unfair Competition Prevention Act now treats unauthorised use of a well-known person's name, likeness or voice "for business purposes" as unfair competition (in force 8 June 2022 by most reports; Press: Managing IP, Shin & Kim, Asia Economy). It is aimed at commercial use. Names and stats are the lower-risk end; faces are the higher.
- A live example of how tight it is: when Gumayusi left T1 in 2025, T1 could not sell shirts with his name "초상권 문제로" (because of portrait rights) (Wiki: Namu, T1/유니폼).

### How others handle it

- **Riot's own Fantasy LCS** ran 2014 to 2018, then closed. In 2020 the LCS said: "For Spring, there will be no LCS endorsed/affiliated products. However, we've created a structure for third-parties to create fantasy products." (Press: Esports News UK.) So licensed third parties get the assets; unlicensed ones do not.
- **Riot's rules today** ban pros from fantasy contests with real-world prizes, and ban fantasy firms from sponsoring community events (Press: esportslegal.news and others). Riot treats fantasy near gambling. A free game with no prizes sits on the safe side of that line; keep it there.
- **FPL**: shirts on the pitch view, licensed photos in lists. The Premier League owns both. It still chose shirts for the pitch, because shirts read faster at small size. (Unverified in a written source; this is how the product looks.)
- **HLTV Fantasy**: uses player photos. HLTV shoots or licenses its own photo library. (Unverified.)
- **gol.gg**: stats tables with team logos, no player photos. **dpm.lol, probuilds**: show pro headshots that look like Riot's; no licence statement found. (All unverified; not opened today.)
- **Wikis**: rely on a named permission from Riot (Liquipedia) or fair use.

### Conclusion

**Can a free fan game show official portraits? Not safely, and not on any written terms.** No source grants reuse. Riot's site terms reserve everything. Riot's fan policy covers Riot's game art for free projects, excludes "games and apps", excludes logos, and cannot license a team's photo or a player's face. Other fan sites that show headshots do so on tolerance, not permission. A free, no-prize, clearly-labelled fan site is unlikely to be chased, but it could be told to remove them at any time and would have no answer.

**On what terms could it?** Only with permission in writing: from Riot/LCK for league photos, or from a team for its own. Asking costs nothing. The 2020 "structure for third parties" suggests Riot has a route, though I could not find its current form.

**The safe fallback, in order of preference:**

1. **Shirt + game name + role icon.** All ours. A flat shirt in the team's colours is a drawing of a colour scheme, not their photo or crest. Keep sponsor marks and crests off it. This is also what FPL does on the pitch.
2. **Initials or the game name set large** in a team-coloured roundel, for lists where a shirt is too wide.
3. **Commissioned illustrations.** Stylised drawings are still the player's likeness, so they lower the copyright risk but not the likeness one. Worth it only with permission.
4. **Champion-style icons.** Riot's champion art is Riot IP, and the "games and apps" line applies. A player's signature champion drawn in our own style is safer than Riot's art, but adds little over the shirt.
5. **A link out** to the player's lolesports.com or Leaguepedia page, where the official photo is. A link is not a copy.

Build the player card so a portrait slot exists but is empty by default. If permission ever comes, the pictures drop in.

## 5. Korean design cues

Most of this section is general knowledge, not read from a page today. Marked **Unverified** as a block. Check anything before it becomes a rule.

### Obangsaek (오방색), the five colours

- Blue (east, wood), red (south, fire), yellow (centre, earth), white (west, metal), black (north, water).
- It maps onto a five-role game without forcing it: five roles, five colours, with the centre colour for mid. It also maps onto the Rift: blue side and red side are already two of the five.
- Use the traditional, slightly muted pigments, not pure RGB. No published hex set is "official".

### Dancheong (단청)

- The painted bands on palace and temple eaves: repeating lotus and geometric units in green, red-orange, blue, white and black, with a strong green ground.
- The cliché is pasting the pattern on as wallpaper. The cue worth taking is the structure: a plain field, with dense pattern only at the ends and edges (the "meoricho" at the beam ends). That is how a table header or a card's top edge could carry it.

### Hangul typography

- Verified today: LoL's Korean site sets Hangul in RixSGo. lolesports.com falls back to Noto Sans KR.
- Free faces that read as modern Korean rather than default: Pretendard (the common product-UI face in Korea, OFL), SUIT, Noto Sans KR, Gmarket Sans (a wide, sporty display face), and Black Han Sans for heavy headlines.
- Korean esports and sports graphics favour heavy, slightly condensed, often italic Hangul for names and scores, with Latin tags (T1, GEN) in a squarer face beside them.
- Which Hangul face the LCK broadcast uses, and each team's custom face: **not found**.
- Player names in the LCK are Latin game names (Faker, Chovy). Hangul is for real names and headings. A card showing both (Faker / 이상혁) is true to how Korean sites do it.

### KBO, K League and Korean sports sites

- Dense tables, small type, many columns. Close to what this project already likes from FM.
- Team colours used as solid blocks behind a team's name, not as thin accents.
- Fixtures shown as a row of day tabs across the top, with the match list beneath.
- Cheer culture shows up in the UI: per-team "응원" (cheer) counts and bars.

### Naver and CHZZK esports pages

- Naver's esports page: a schedule strip by date, each match a row with both crests, score in the middle, and a "승부예측" (predict the winner) bar splitting in the two teams' colours.
- CHZZK: dark theme, a bright green accent (Naver's green), live badges, chat to the right of the video.
- For 2026 the LCK and CHZZK added the win-probability graph and match timeline to the stream (Press, above).

## What this suggests for the screens

1. **Rift view layout.** Draw the map as a diamond: our side's base bottom-left, river top-left to bottom-right, three lanes. Place the five shirts where the roles stand: top, jungle, mid, bot, support. Keep our own colours for terrain; none are published.
2. **Shirt icon recipe.** One flat shirt shape, three fills: body, trim (collar and cuffs), and a side or shoulder panel. That is enough to split the three reds: T1 white body with red trim and black shoulders; KT black with red; NS black with deeper red and gold. Game name under the shirt, as on the real ones. No crest, no sponsor.
3. **Team colours as data.** Store body, trim and panel per team per season, with the source beside each. Three teams were renamed this year and two kits changed mid-season. Nothing here should be a constant.
4. **Role icons: draw our own, on Riot's idea.** A square frame for the map; light the top-left corner for top, the diagonal for mid, the bottom-right corner for bot. Jungle and support get their own simple glyphs. Do not ship Riot's SVGs.
5. **Borrow the client's two-tone habit, not its palette.** Lit part bright, the rest at half strength. A dark ground near `#010A13` with parchment text near `#F0E6D2` reads as LoL at once; one gold for frames is plenty. Full hextech gold-and-blue everywhere would look like a Riot product, which the policy says to avoid.
6. **Side colours.** Blue side left, red side right, always, in fixtures and match rows. The LCK's own pair is a violet-blue `#6454ED` and a red `#F0383B`; choosing near those, not pure blue and red, will feel like the LCK and not a generic MOBA.
7. **Fonts.** Do not use Beaufort or Spiegel. Pretendard covers Latin and Hangul in one family and is free. Show the game name large in Latin with the Hangul real name small beneath.
8. **Portrait slot, empty by default.** Shirt, game name and role icon carry the player card. Leave a slot a licensed picture could fill later. Link out to the official page for the face.
9. **One Korean cue, used structurally.** Either the five obangsaek colours as the five role colours, or a dancheong-style patterned band only at a panel's top edge. Not both, and no taegeuk, hanbok or temple roofs.
10. **Footer line and no logos.** Carry Riot's required disclaimer sentence. Write "LCK" and team names as plain text. No LCK logo, no LoL logo, no team crests, until someone says yes in writing.
