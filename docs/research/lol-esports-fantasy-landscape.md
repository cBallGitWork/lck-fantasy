# FPL rules leave Europe's LoL fantasy open

Yes, there is room. As of late October 2026 the searches found **no free, English-language game that runs FPL's rules (fixed budget, squad by role, limited free transfers with -4 hits, captain, chips, mini-leagues) for the LEC or the LCK**. Riot's own budget game covers only the Americas. The one LEC game found, Rifty, is a Spanish market-and-buyout app with about 5,000 users. The opening is real but small. Riot's season-long Fantasy LCS lost more than 90% of its players before it closed, and Riot's 2025 return in the Americas drew about 75,000 players in its first week, under 1% of FPL's scale. A hobby LEC game should therefore aim for a community of friends' leagues, Discord servers and Reddit regulars, not mass reach. It can be built for free from an unofficial lolesports feed plus Leaguepedia and Oracle's Elixir, but only while Riot tolerates it. It has to stay free, with no cash prizes, under both Riot's policies and UK prize-competition law. The stream overlay is feasible, but a second-screen page with a pop-out mini window gets most of its value for much less work and spoiler risk. **One caveat applies to everything below: the session's network blocked nearly every primary page (lolesports.com, Riot's support and developer sites, Leaguepedia, Oracle's Elixir, GRID, the Chrome Web Store, Wikipedia and most news sites). The findings rest on search-result snippets, apart from one OpenAPI spec read in full from raw GitHub, and every specific number, date and policy line needs checking against its source before you rely on it.**

**Contents:** the gap in Europe and Korea · what happened to season-long fantasy · data and its limits · overlays and second screens · the LEC and LCK calendars · an LCK skin · where the opening is · conclusion

## The LEC and LCK have no FPL-rules game, and Riot's game stops at the Americas

Riot's only live fantasy product is the Americas one. **LTA Fantasy opened as a free beta on 2 April 2025**: a weekly lineup of LTA pros on a **50-gold budget**, prices that move with form, automatic public leagues, unlimited custom leagues and in-game skins as prizes ([LoL Esports](https://lolesports.com/en-US/news/lta-fantasy-is-here); [Riot FAQ](https://support.riotgames.com/en-us/league-of-legends/events/lta-fantasy-faq)). It returned for Split 3 after what Strafe called a "successful debut" ([Strafe](https://www.strafe.com/news/read/lta-fantasy-returns-for-split-3-after-its-successful-debut/)). For 2026 it was split into separate LCS and CBLOL apps, and the LCS one was rebuilt as a league hub with a planned **19 January 2026** launch ([esports.gg](https://esports.gg/news/league-of-legends/lcs-2026-format-updates/); [Strafe](https://www.strafe.com/news/read/lcs-2026-season-brings-new-americas-cup-road-shows-fantasy-update-and-more/)). An LCS Fantasy rules page exists, which suggests the app shipped, but it could not be read ([lcsofficial.gg](https://lcsofficial.gg/en/rules)). Outside the Americas, Riot runs only Pick'Em bracket predictions for internationals ([EarlyGame](https://earlygame.com/news/gaming/grab-skins-and-emotes-worlds-2025-pickems-are-here-heres-everything-you-need-to-know)).

Riot's game is the closest thing to FPL, but it is a **weekly** budget lineup rather than a persistent squad. The snippets mention no transfer limits, hits or chips. Its scoring averages a player's points across the games of a weekend rather than summing them ([Riot FAQ](https://support.riotgames.com/en-us/league-of-legends/events/lta-fantasy-faq)). Its only captain-like twist is a team-diversity bonus of +10% for players from three different teams ([LoL Esports fantasy hub](https://lolesports.com/en-US/news/fantasy)).

In Europe the only confirmed current game is **Rifty**. It is a free Spanish app that bills itself as the official fantasy of the LES (Spain's league), lets managers pick LEC players too, and claims "over 5,000 users". It follows the Spanish Biwenger/Comunio model, with a player market, value-history graphs and "Pay clause" buyouts between managers ([App Store](https://apps.apple.com/ca/app/rifty/id6744029216)). That is a persistent squad, but not FPL's economy. Sleeper ran draft-style LCS/LEC fantasy from 2020 and was the official LCS partner from 2021 ([Sleeper blog](https://blog.sleeper.app/fantasy-league-of-legends-available-today/); [win.gg](https://win.gg/news/fantasy-lcs-is-back-for-2021-season-and-this-is-how-it-works/)). The last sighting found is a Samsung-sponsored season in January 2023 ([Esports Advocate](https://esportsadvocate.net/2023/01/lcs-2023-partners/)), and its 2025–26 status is unknown. The paid market is US daily fantasy and props: DraftKings slates across the LCK, LPL, LEC and LCS, and PrizePicks props, mostly documented in 2020–22 ([RotoWire](https://www.rotowire.com/esports/article/dfs-101-how-to-play-league-of-legends-on-draftkings-50118); [Fantasy Alarm](https://www.fantasyalarm.com/articles/esports/prize-picks/prizepicks-league-of-legends-top-plays-lec-july-22/130627)). None of it is season-long, and none of it suits a free UK game. **For the LCK, nothing beyond US daily fantasy turned up**, though only one Korean-language search was run.

| Product | Leagues | Model | Status (late 2026) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Riot LTA → LCS / CBLOL Fantasy | Americas only | Weekly 50-gold lineup, skins as prizes | Live, LCS app planned for Jan 2026 (launch not confirmed) |
| Rifty | LES + LEC | Spanish market with buyout clauses | Live, ~5k users claimed |
| Sleeper | LCS (+ LEC) | Season-long head-to-head draft | Unknown after 2023 |
| SuperFantasy (LVP) | LEC | Card packs, Ultimate-Team style | Seen in 2020–21 coverage, current status unknown |
| DraftKings / PrizePicks | LCK, LPL, LEC, LCS | Real-money daily contests and props | Mostly 2020–22 evidence |
| **FPL-rules season game** | **LEC / LCK** | **Budget, transfers, hits, chips** | **None found** |

Two blind spots weaken the "none found" verdict. Reddit and Show HN could not be searched properly, so a small indie game could exist unnoticed. A manual check of r/lolesports, r/LEC and r/FantasyLCS is cheap and worth doing before building. And Riot could extend its LCS app to Europe at any time. Riot running its own game in a region would be the single biggest threat to a hobby product there.

## Season-long fantasy shed 90% of its players, but weekly games and Pick'Em still pull crowds

The history is a warning about retention, not about interest. Riot's free, prize-less Fantasy LCS ran from the 2014 Summer Split, drafting from both the NA and EU LCS ([Riot FAQ](https://support.riotgames.com/en-us/league-of-legends/events/fantasy-lcs-faq)). Europe never had a separate Riot fantasy. By December 2018 Riot had taken it offline: commissioner Chris Greeley admitted fantasy "hasn't been prioritized" ([Upcomer](https://upcomer.com/riot-games-changing-fantasy-lcs)). In May 2019 Riot said the **user base had fallen by more than 90% from its first year** ([Nexus](https://nexus.leagueoflegends.com/en-us/2019/05/2019-summer-split-fantasy-update/); [win.gg](https://win.gg/news/fantasy-lcs-and-lec-postponed-beyond-the-2019-summer-split/)). In December 2019 it handed fantasy to third parties ([Inven Global](https://www.invenglobal.com/articles/10031/lcslec-fantasy-is-returning-in-spring-2020-the-perfect-christmas-gift-for-pro-league-fans)). The sources cannot separate genuine fading interest from Riot neglecting the product, and Riot itself blamed the neglect. Europe then got LVP's **SuperFantasy**, a free card game that sold random packs and played "more like Ultimate Team from FIFA than a traditional fantasy experience" ([esports.net](https://www.esports.net/fantasy/lec); [ESTNN](https://estnn.com/league-of-legends-lec-launches-superfantasy)).

The 2025 comeback gives the best current scale anchor. A Brazilian outlet reported **more than 35,000 sign-ups in about 24 hours, about 75,000 active players by the end of the first round and about 9,000 leagues** for LTA Fantasy ([JWave](https://www.jwave.com.br/2025/04/lta-fantasy-estreia-75-mil-jogadores-9-mil-ligas/)). That came with Riot's own promotion, Riot ID login and skins as prizes, across two regions. FPL reported a record **11.5 million** players ([Inside World Football](https://www.insideworldfootball.com/?p=164780)), about 150 times more. Low-effort, event-based prediction does far better still: Worlds Pick'Em reportedly reached **more than 6–7 million** in 2021 ([mein-mmo](https://mein-mmo.de/en/lol-of-over-7-4-million-players-only-a-genius-has-gotten-everything-right-at-the-worlds,743383)). Paid esports daily fantasy never got past niche. DraftKings called LoL "a very niche offering" in 2016 ([Legal Sports Report](https://www.legalsportsreport.com/8663/draftkings-esports-sponsorships/)), and FanDuel's AlphaDraft closed in October 2016 ([ESPN](https://africa.espn.com/esports/story/_/id/17835154/alphadraft-fantasy-gambling-website-closes-doors)).

Read together, these figures say demand runs in this order: inside-the-client predictions (millions), then a weekly budget game backed by the league (tens of thousands), then a season-long draft game (fell away). A hobby LEC game without Riot's promotion or skin prizes should expect hundreds to low thousands of players. That is a fine size for mini-leagues among friends, and it means the product has to be pleasant to come back to every week rather than built to grow. The steelman case against building is that Riot's 90% decline shows a season-long LoL game fails once the novelty wears off. The counter is that FPL's hooks (transfers, hits, chips, rank movement) are exactly the weekly re-engagement loop Riot's draft game lacked. Neither side has data here.

## Free data exists, but only while Riot tolerates it

**Riot's official live data is commercial-only.** Riot's data site says live, fixture and audio-visual data "are only available for commercial purposes through GRID", and that a non-commercial LoL Esports Data Portal is still being rolled out ([riotesportsdata.com](https://riotesportsdata.com/en-us/league-of-legends/)). GRID has been Riot's exclusive LoL data partner since a deal announced in December 2023, reportedly for three years, with Riot taking an equity stake ([Sportico](https://www.sportico.com/business/tech/2023/riot-games-grid-data-deal-1234748582/); [SVG](https://www.sportsvideo.org/2023/12/04/riot-games-and-grid-announce-exclusive-global-esports-data-partnership/)). If that term runs from early 2024, the deal comes up around the end of 2026, which makes the next few months worth watching. GRID's free Open Access tier covers only CS2 and Dota 2 historical data, not LoL ([GRID](https://grid.gg/what-is-grid-open-access/)). The public Riot developer API does not cover pro games, because those are played on tournament servers ([FloPrm/lol_analytics](https://github.com/FloPrm/lol_analytics)).

Three free sources fill the gap, each with a different job:

| Source | What it gives | Cadence | Risk |
|---|---|---|---|
| Unofficial lolesports API (`esports-api.lolesports.com` + `feed.lolesports.com/livestats/v1`) | Schedules, standings, rosters; per-player K/D/A, CS, gold, level, wards, items, kill participation, damage *share*; team towers, dragons, barons | ~10 s frames, recent frames only | Unsanctioned, shared web key, can change without notice |
| Leaguepedia Cargo API | Post-match player stats, picks and bans, rosters, tournaments | Volunteer-edited (latency unmeasured) | Licence and rate limits unverified |
| Oracle's Elixir CSVs | Match data for LEC, LCK, LPL and more, from 2020 | Daily | LPL feed broke in July 2026; licence unverified |

The lolesports endpoints come from the one primary source read in full: an MIT-licensed community spec that lists the gateway endpoints, the shared `x-api-key` scraped from the web client, and the livestats `/window` and `/details` fields ([openapi.yaml](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/vickz84259/lolesports-api-docs/master/openapi.yaml)). Projects in 2026 still use the feed, polling about every 12 seconds and warning that "Riot can change or rate-limit it" ([lol-predictor-app](https://github.com/ThomasCHOOCHOO/lol-predictor-app); [worlds-2026-live](https://github.com/hyderoo/worlds-2026-live)). Older lolesports endpoints have broken before ([hextechdocs](https://www.hextechdocs.dev/gathering-lolesports-data/)). The feed carries everything an FPL-style scoring table needs except vision score, absolute damage and first-blood flags. Because it keeps only recent frames, the scorer must store frames itself or settle final scores from a post-match source. A live probe of the endpoints was refused in this session, so **whether the key and fields still work in October 2026 is untested**. Leaguepedia is queried through Cargo, with client libraries in TypeScript ([poro](https://github.com/pacexy/poro)) and Python ([mwrogue](https://mwrogue.readthedocs.io/en/latest/mwrogue.html)). Oracle's Elixir publishes free daily CSVs ([Oracle's Elixir](https://oracleselixir.com/tools/downloads)), but its LPL data stopped for about two weeks in July–August 2026 because of an upstream problem ([OE blog](https://oracleselixir.com/blog/post/14040/lpl-data-is-back-and-better)). A Kaggle mirror labels its data CC BY-NC-SA 4.0, a third-party label rather than OE's own terms ([Kaggle](https://www.kaggle.com/datasets/lauffing/oracles-elixir-league-of-legends-pro-play-data)). The sensible architecture is to use the live feed for provisional in-game points and settle official gameweek scores from a post-match source with a cross-check. That mirrors FPL's own "provisional bonus, then confirmed" pattern.

For the repo rule "persist the season-stable `code`, never the per-season id", the feed's `esportsPlayerId` and Leaguepedia's player pages (which follow renames through redirects) are the candidate stable keys. That is reasoning from familiarity with those sources, not something read this session.

**Riot's policies leave a free, no-prize game possible but precarious.** Riot's developer policies say a product "cannot feature betting or gambling functionality", on pain of losing API access or "legal recourse" ([Riot General Policies](https://developer.riotgames.com/policies/general)). No policy found names fantasy explicitly. Riot's broadcast terms and its NA tournament sponsor rules, however, group "fantasy sports or esports" operators with gambling ([Riot Broadcast Terms](https://tod.riotgames.com/broadcast-terms); [NA Tournaments](https://support-developer.riotgames.com/hc/en-us/articles/22698775429395-NA-Tournaments)). Riot's fan-content policy, "Legal Jibber Jabber", allows Riot IP only in non-commercial projects, and Riot can withdraw permission "at any time, for any reason or no reason" ([Riot Legal](https://www.riotgames.com/en/legal)). The full text was not read, so its exceptions for ads or donations are unconfirmed. On the brand side, team and player names used as facts are generally safe. Team logos belong to the teams, not Riot, so the safe default is to leave them out. That is a general trademark reading, not a sourced Riot statement.

**UK gambling law draws the line at money in.** Law-firm summaries say a paid competition avoids licensing only if real skill would stop "a significant proportion" of people entering or winning, or if it offers an equally prominent free entry route. Otherwise a paid game of chance is a lottery that needs a licence ([Irwin Mitchell](https://www.irwinmitchell.com/business/licensing/betting-gaming/prize-draws); [Northridge Law](https://northridgelaw.com/gambling-regulatory-update-operating-a-prize-competition-in-great-britain/)). A fantasy-tools site warns the Gambling Commission may treat pooled-entry fantasy contests as pool betting ([Premier Fantasy Tools](https://www.premierfantasytools.com/fantasy-vs-gambling-in-europe-what-fantasy-football-managers-should-actually-know/)). No Gambling Commission page was read directly. The practical conclusion is clear all the same. "Free to enter, no cash or cash-equivalent prizes, data sources credited" keeps the game outside UK gambling rules and inside Riot's fan tolerance. A "£10 each, winner takes the pot" mini-league run through the site is the riskiest shape on both fronts, and anything involving money needs legal advice first.

## A stream overlay works, but a pop-out second screen gets most of the value

**The technical pattern is already proven, and the fantasy layer is not.** Small Chrome extensions already paint LoL esports live stats over Twitch and YouTube from the lolesports feed. RORR shows gold-difference graphs and objective timelines ([Chrome Web Store](https://chromewebstore.google.com/detail/rorr/aeijenbcnhhmcgknnimiddbdgpndfcpn)), and StreamViz claims to work in fullscreen ([Chrome Web Store](https://chromewebstore.google.com/detail/streamviz-for-lol/ckgnjpjlolkggdmlbaogldjdmcendnjn?hl=en)). Costream Override shows an extension can rewrite the player embed on lolesports.com ([chromeboard](https://chromeboard.com/extension/costream-override-pgbjdbbihnobbnpipolpmebdfelkboek)). The only "your fantasy team over a stream" product found is a football ticker for Yahoo, ESPN and Sleeper with **about 128 users** ([extscope](https://extscope.org/extension/dbhhoddpcmjohmmfjdiaingjfgmpieog)), which suggests weak organic demand for overlays in particular. Riot and Twitch built a "Live Tracker" Twitch Extension in 2020, but it tracked streamers' own games and only streamers could enable it ([Twitch blog](https://blog.twitch.tv/en/2020/02/20/live-tracker-extension-for-league-of-legends-get-live-and-on-demand-stats-for-league-matches)). The Chrome Web Store listings could not be opened, so user counts, ratings and whether these extensions apply any stream delay are unverified.

The build itself is standard Manifest V3 work. Content scripts on twitch.tv, youtube.com and lolesports.com mount a Shadow-DOM overlay inside the player container, so it survives fullscreen, and mutation observers handle each site rebuilding its player on navigation. Since Chrome 116, WebSocket traffic keeps an extension service worker alive past its 30-second idle limit ([Chrome docs](https://developer.chrome.com/docs/extensions/mv3/tut_websockets?authuser=3)). The cleanest design computes fantasy points on your own backend and sends each user one small payload. The awkward parts are lolesports.com's cross-origin player iframes, which need wider host permissions, and the ongoing maintenance as Twitch and YouTube change their pages.

**The hard problem is spoilers, not code.** Official broadcasts run on a deliberate delay for competitive integrity. One LCL final was replayed because an unofficial stream had none ([Inven Global](https://www.invenglobal.com/lol/articles/11076/the-lcl-finals-remade-game-1-due-to-an-unofficial-stream-having-no-delay)). PandaScore puts typical Twitch and YouTube esports delay at **3–4 minutes** behind the game server ([PandaScore](https://developers.pandascore.co/docs/low-latency-feed-overview)). Riot's actual delay per league in 2026 was not found, nor how far the lolesports feed itself lags the server. An overlay that shows points as soon as the feed updates will reveal kills and Barons minutes before viewers see them. Even a running team total spoils, because a jump tells you something happened. Twitch Extensions get the viewer's latency to the broadcaster (`hlsLatencyBroadcaster`), but it explicitly excludes any delay added at the broadcaster's end ([Twitch dev forum](https://discuss.dev.twitch.tv/t/synchronize-extension-with-the-video-stream/19482)), so not even an official extension can sync on its own. The workable design buffers feed frames by their `rfc460Timestamp`. It shows each frame at "now minus (league broadcast delay + player latency + a user nudge)", starts from a cautious default, and offers a calibration tap ("a kill just happened") plus a "show live, risk spoilers" toggle. VODs break any wall-clock offset and need the video's position anchored to game start. Mainstream sports apps sidestep the problem with a plain "hide scores" switch ([YouTube TV](https://support.google.com/youtubetv/answer/7641863?hl=en); [NBA](https://support.watch.nba.com/hc/en-us/articles/115000587174)).

On policy, viewer-side overlays on Twitch and YouTube are an accepted Chrome Web Store category, provided the extension keeps to its single purpose and narrow permissions ([Chrome single-purpose policy](https://developer.chrome.com/docs/extensions/mv3/single_purpose/)). Because an extension only paints on the viewer's own screen, it is not a co-stream and needs no Twitch or Riot approval. That is reasoning; the current Twitch, YouTube and Chrome policy texts could not be read. The bigger Riot risk remains the unofficial feed, not the overlay.

**The lower-friction route is a second-screen web page with a pop-out mini window.** Document Picture-in-Picture lets a web page open an always-on-top mini window of any content. It has shipped in Chrome and Edge since version 116 ([Chrome docs](https://developer.chrome.com/docs/web-platform/document-picture-in-picture)), but not in Safari or on mobile, and Firefox support is disputed ([RabbitPair](https://www.rabbitpair.com/en/products/dualpip/faqs/document-pip-browser-limits)). The pop-out floats over Twitch, YouTube, lolesports.com, Kick (an official platform since MSI 2026 ([u.gg](https://u.gg/lol/news/riot-games-adds-kick-to-global-esports-streaming-pool))) and co-streams alike. It needs no extension install, no per-site page hacks and no store review. The same page works on a phone, which is plausibly where most fantasy managers already look during a match. It cannot read the stream's latency, so it relies on the delay slider, which the extension needs anyway. A Twitch Extension is only worth it if Riot or a big co-streamer agrees to install it.

For **2027, only one esports change is confirmed**: LEC Versus will not return and the league goes back to a "classic" 10-team winter format ([Sheep Esports](https://www.sheepesports.com/en/articles/artem-bykov-we-are-not-bringing-lec-versus-back/en)). Alongside that, the LEC salary cap drops to €1.65M ([Strafe](https://www.strafe.com/news/read/lec-lowers-team-salary-cap-for-2027/)), and the game itself gets Riot's "League Next" overhaul at an unannounced point in the year ([PC Gamer](https://www.pcgamer.com/games/moba/a-major-overhaul-of-league-of-legends-is-reportedly-coming-in-2027-once-were-done-it-should-be-the-best-time-ever-to-get-your-friends-into-league)). The projection, which is not confirmed, is Winter (January to early March, possibly Bo1 as in 2025 ([Wikipedia 2025 LEC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_LEC_season))), then First Stand, Spring (late March to June), MSI and EWC, Summer (late July to September), and Worlds. The LCK most likely repeats Cup, Rounds 1–2, Road to MSI, Rounds 3–4 and Playoffs. Riot usually publishes the official calendar around Worlds or December, so check again in November–December 2026 before fixing gameweeks.

What this means for the game design is reasoning built on those snippets:

**Seasons and gameweeks.** The LEC is not one 38-week season. It is three blocks of five to nine weeks, separated by gaps of three to seven weeks for internationals. That suggests a "season" per split, each with its own chips, or one annual game that pauses through the gaps. Riot's own game resets per split. A mid-season wildcard at each split boundary would handle roster changes between splits, which are common in LoL.

**Deadlines.** For the LEC alone, a gameweek deadline of Friday around 16:00 UK suits Summer's Friday–Monday schedule. In weeks that start on Saturday it can move to Saturday afternoon. The LCK plays in the UK morning, Wednesday to Sunday. A combined game would need a Wednesday ~08:00 UK deadline covering Wednesday to Monday, which is awkward but workable.

**Double and blank gameweeks.** These happen naturally. In LEC Spring and Summer each team plays one or two Bo3 series a week, so "doubles" occur constantly without any fixture-list quirk. A Bo3 can also last two or three games, which adds variance even within a week. The playoffs take six of ten teams, so four teams blank for two to three weeks. Those weeks, plus internationals where perhaps two of ten teams are playing, are where Free Hit and Bench Boost decisions become interesting. An international event could run as an optional bonus gameweek rather than part of the main race.

**Scoring.** Three facts drive it. First, games average **32–34 minutes and 27–29 kills** across the LEC and LCK, and LEC Versus's Bo1 games looked statistically no different from Spring's Bo3 games ([gol.gg Versus](https://gol.gg/tournament/tournament-stats/LEC%202026%20Versus%20Season/); [gol.gg Spring](https://gol.gg/tournament/tournament-stats/LEC%202026%20Spring%20Season/)). Format changes how many games are played, not what happens in each one. Second, the LCK plays roughly twice the LEC's regular-season volume (**204 games in Rounds 1–2 against 111 in LEC Spring**), so summed points would swamp LEC players in any mixed game ([gol.gg LCK](https://gol.gg/tournament/tournament-stats/LCK%202026%20Rounds%201-2/)). Third, hard fearless draft applies to every Tier 1 Bo3 and Bo5 ([esports.gg](https://esports.gg/news/league-of-legends/fearless-draft-in-lol-esports-explained/)), so game 3 is played on depleted champion pools. Riot answered the series-length problem by averaging per game. An FPL-style game has to choose deliberately between summing (rewards volume, makes doubles meaningful), averaging (removes series-length luck, kills doubles) or a capped middle option. Role balance also matters. Supports get few kills and many assists, and mid and ADC players carry kills, so an unweighted table would make carry slots dominate. That is general knowledge; per-role averages need pulling from Oracle's Elixir before setting weights. **League Next may shift the meta in 2027**, so every scoring weight belongs in league data, not constants, which matches the repo's existing "league rules are data" rule.

**Squad shape.** A role-based squad (top, jungle, mid, ADC, support, perhaps a coach or team slot) drawn from a pool of about 50 starters will converge on a template much faster than FPL's 600-plus players. Price changes, captaincy and chip timing must carry more of the differentiation. Substitutes and emergency signings exist under the rules (a 2024 LEC rulebook gives teams an hour on match day to field one ([LEC 2024 Rulebook](https://static.wikia.nocookie.net/lolesports_gamepedia_en/images/2/29/LEC_2024_Rulebook.pdf))), so auto-subs need a rule for a starter who is benched.

## An LCK skin with invented sponsors is a presentation layer, not a ruleset (reasoning, not research)

No research was done on this idea, so this section is design reasoning only. The useful split is between **rules and skin**. The LCK version should run the same engine, scoring and chips as the LEC game, configured as data for the LCK's Wednesday–Sunday weeks and higher game volume. The Korean theme (language touches, LoL community in-jokes, cultural references, fictional sponsor boards) should live entirely in a swappable presentation layer. Then a theme change never touches scoring, the draft or saved league data, and a plain skin is always available. That keeps the "some breakage is acceptable only for the look" line intact.

Invented sponsors are where it can go wrong. LCK teams carry real corporate backing (the Korean telecom rivalry Sheep Esports headlined on opening day ([Sheep Esports](https://www.sheepesports.com/articles/the-lck-returns-on-april-1-with-a-telecom-war-on-opening-day-and-nine-weeks-of-competition/en)) is one example). A fake sponsor that reads as a parody of a real team backer, its logo, colours or a near-miss name, risks trademark complaints and looks like mockery of the companies that fund the league. The safe pattern is wholly original names with no sound-alikes of real Korean conglomerates, telecoms, banks or food brands, and a visible "fictional sponsors" note. The same caution applies to Riot IP. Champion art, the LCK logo and Riot fonts fall under Jibber Jabber's revocable non-commercial permission, and team logos are the teams' trademarks. Original illustration and type are safer than borrowed assets. Finally, humour about another culture is easy to misjudge from outside it. Have a Korean LoL fan review the copy, the jokes and any Hangul before launch, and pay or credit them. A theme meant as affection can land as caricature.

## Where the opening is, and what to build first

The gap is specific: an **English-language, free, FPL-rules, season-long LEC game**. Nobody else serves it (going by what searches could see), it suits a UK schedule and it reuses FPL knowledge the user already has. The LCK is the second market, with more games, Korean-time mornings and no competitor found, but a more demanding audience to theme for. The constraints are equally specific: data at Riot's tolerance, no money changing hands and a realistic audience in the hundreds to low thousands.

| Priority | Recommendation | Why |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Verify the unknowns before building: hit the lolesports gateway and livestats endpoints, read the Leaguepedia and Oracle's Elixir licence terms, search Reddit for indie competitors, and check whether Riot's LCS app shipped and has spread to Europe | Everything here rests on snippets; these four checks decide go or no-go |
| 2 | Build the LEC game first as a web app: budget, role squad, free transfers with -4 hits, captain, a small chip set, mini-leagues; free, no prizes, data sources credited, no team logos or Riot art | Fills the confirmed gap with the lowest legal and policy exposure |
| 3 | Settle official scores from a post-match source, using the live feed only for provisional points, and store frames yourself | The feed keeps only recent frames and can vanish; scoring must survive that |
| 4 | Keep scoring weights, gameweek boundaries, deadlines and chip rules as league data; decide sum vs average per game deliberately and set role weights from Oracle's Elixir history | Bo3 variance, fearless, the LCK's volume and League Next all argue against constants |
| 5 | Design around splits: three LEC blocks with wildcards at split boundaries, pause or bonus weeks for internationals, a Friday ~16:00 UK deadline; confirm the 2027 calendar in Nov–Dec 2026 | Matches the real calendar instead of forcing FPL's 38 weeks |
| 6 | Ship a live second-screen page with a delay slider, calibration tap and "hide scores" default, plus a Document PiP pop-out on desktop Chromium | Most of the overlay's value at near-zero policy and maintenance cost |
| 7 | Build the Chrome extension only if users ask for it; limit host permissions to the stream sites and your API, and reuse the second screen's delay buffer | Proven but costly to maintain, and overlay demand looks thin |
| 8 | Add the LCK as a second league on the same engine, with the Korean theme as a removable skin, wholly original sponsors and a Korean fan's review | Expands reach without forking rules or risking brand and IP trouble |
| 9 | Get legal advice before any entry fee, prize or sponsor money | UK prize-competition law and Riot's gambling and commercial lines both change at that point |

## Conclusion

The most useful shift from this research is that the main risk is data access, not competition. The market gap for FPL rules in Europe looks clear, but the whole product rests on an unofficial feed that Riot fences off for commercial use and could close without notice. The end of the GRID deal around the end of 2026, and the promised non-commercial data portal, could improve or worsen that, so a hobby build should be designed to degrade gracefully to post-match scoring. The second shift is that LoL's split calendar is an asset, not a mismatch. Natural doubles, playoff blanks and international breaks give chips and transfers more texture per week than a football season does, provided the scoring decides deliberately how series length and the LCK's volume count.

The history says to measure success by whether leagues of friends still play in week eight, not by sign-ups. Riot's own season-long game lost nine in ten players while Riot ignored it. A small, well-tended FPL-style LEC game that keeps its weekly loop alive is a different proposition, and the honest next step is the cheap verification work in recommendation 1, because every figure in this report came through a blocked network and a search-snippet keyhole.
