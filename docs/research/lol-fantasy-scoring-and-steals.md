## 1. Bottom line

No other LoL fantasy game uses whole low numbers. Riot's current games (LCS, CBLOL, LTA) share one table of decimals and about 25 lines: kill +1.5, assist +1, death −1, CS +0.01, plus threshold, role and steal bonuses. DraftKings and Underdog use kill +3, assist +2, death −1, CS +0.02. None of them fully stops long games paying more. Riot comes closest: it averages a player's games per weekend, uses rate stats taken at 14 or 15 minutes, and pays a "stomp" bonus for wins under 27 minutes. Flol's best-2-games rule has a direct precedent: Riot's 2016 Fantasy LCS let leagues count "first two games" or "best game". Riot noted the catch that applies to Flol too: players in longer series get an extra draw. For steals, the good news is firm. Riot's own match data records `objectivesStolen` per player, and Leaguepedia publishes that data for LCK games, free, within 15 minutes to 15 hours of the game ending. Riot's LCS table already pays +4 for a stolen Baron or Elder and +2 for a stolen Dragon or Herald. A "teamfight clutch" bonus can be built from the same data's kill timeline, but only after the game. No standard definition of a teamfight exists, so Flol would have to set its own.

## 2. Riot's current games share one decimal, bonus-heavy table

Riot runs one fantasy engine across the Americas. **LCS Fantasy 2026 and CBLOL Fantasy 2026 print the same table** ([LCS rules](https://lcsofficial.gg/en/rules), [CBLOL rules](https://cblol.gg/pt/rules)). LTA Fantasy 2025 used an earlier version of it ([Strafe](https://www.strafe.com/news/read/lta-fantasy-returns-for-split-3-after-its-successful-debut/)). No official LCK, LPL or LEC fantasy with a points table was found.

| Line | LCS / CBLOL 2026 | Old Fantasy LCS (2015) |
|---|---|---|
| Kill | +1.5 | +2 |
| Assist | +1 | +1.5 |
| Death | −1 | −0.5 |
| CS | +0.01 each | +0.01 each |
| First blood | +1 | team stat |
| Win | +1 | team stat |
| Multikills | triple +2, quadra +3, penta +5 | triple +2, quadra +5, penta +10 |
| Big game | 10+ kills +3 | 10+ kills +2, 10+ assists +2 |
| Other | KP ≥70% +2; damage share ≥30% +3; stomp win +2; perfect game +3; +1 per 1,000 gold lead at 14:00 | none |
| Steals | Baron or Elder +4; Dragon or Herald +2 | none |

Sources: [LCS rules](https://lcsofficial.gg/en/rules), [CBLOL rules](https://cblol.gg/pt/rules), [lcsfans blog, Jan 2015](https://lcsfans.blogspot.com/search/label/Fantasy%20LCS).

CBLOL defines a **stomp as a 10k+ gold lead or a game under 27 minutes**, and a perfect game as 0 deaths and KDA ≥5 ([CBLOL rules](https://cblol.gg/pt/rules)). Each role then gets its own bonuses. Top gets +1 per solo kill and +2 for tanking 25% of team damage. Jungle gets +1.5 if the team takes 4+ Dragons and +2 per Baron. Mid and ADC get +1.5 for 10 CS a minute at 15:00. **Supports get +2 for 10+ assists, +2 for KP ≥75%, +1.5 for first Dragon, and points equal to vision score per minute** ([LCS rules](https://lcsofficial.gg/en/rules)).

There is no captain. Instead, a manager predicts each player's champion for a ×1.3 to ×1.7 multiplier, and gets +5% to +25% for picking players from 2 to 6 teams ([CBLOL rules](https://cblol.gg/pt/rules)). **There is no team slot either.** In 2026 the LCS and CBLOL added a coach whose score is the average of his roster's players ([LCS rules](https://lcsofficial.gg/en/rules)). The old Fantasy LCS did have a team slot: win +2, first blood +2, dragon +1, baron +2, tower +1 ([lcsfans blog](https://lcsfans.blogspot.com/search/label/Fantasy%20LCS)). Flol's coach-as-team, scored on wins, is closer to that old team slot than to Riot's 2026 coach.

Riot gave no reasons for its 2025 values. It shut Fantasy LCS in December 2018 at about 10% of peak participation, saying the product "needed an overhaul" ([Riot Nexus](https://nexus.leagueoflegends.com/en-us/2018/12/the-future-of-fantasy/), summary only).

## 3. DraftKings and the prop sites scale up the old Fantasy LCS table

**DraftKings scores per game: kill +3, assist +2, death −1, CS +0.02, and +2 for 10 kills or 10 assists.** Its team slot gets turret +1, dragon +2, baron +3, first blood +2, win +2 and +2 for a win under 30 minutes. The captain scores 1.5× ([DraftKings rules](https://www.draftkings.com/help/rules/league-of-legends), search summary only; the page refused direct reading). No multikill bonus showed in that summary. The roster is six players and one team, under a $50,000 cap ([RotoWire](https://www.rotowire.com/esports/article/dfs-101-how-to-play-league-of-legends-on-draftkings-50118)).

DraftKings handles a short series bluntly. **A 2-0 sweep in a Bo3 pays +20 per player "in lieu of points from a third game"** ([RotoWire](https://www.rotowire.com/esports/article/dfs-101-how-to-play-league-of-legends-on-draftkings-50118)). That rewards the sweep rather than the long series.

Underdog uses the same kill 3 / assist 2 / death −1 / CS 0.02 values ([Underdog](https://help.underdogfantasy.com/en/articles/10905524-pick-em-scoring-esports)). The prop sites solve series length by **fixing the counted maps**. Underdog says "In a Maps 1-2 projection, Map 3 does not count". Chalkboard sells "Map 1" and "Map 1+2" lines and counts only those maps ([Chalkboard](https://helps.chalkboard.io/en/articles/11584866-e-sports-scoring-rules)). DraftKings Pick6 counts "Guaranteed Maps only" ([Pick6](https://pick6.draftkings.com/pick6-rules-and-scoring-league-of-legends)). No exact PrizePicks formula was found.

Other esports normalise length differently. HLTV's CS fantasy runs on its per-round player rating, so map length washes out by construction ([HLTV Rating 3.0](https://www.hltv.org/news/42485/introducing-rating-30)). Valve's Dota fantasy uses GPM, a per-minute stat ([Blix](https://blix.gg/?p=11996)). Neither gives whole numbers.

## 4. Nobody truly stops long games paying more; five levers exist

Every table above still pays raw kills, assists and CS, and all three grow with game length. The games use five levers against that. No published analysis measured how well any of them works.

| Lever | Who uses it | What it fixes |
|---|---|---|
| Fast-win bonus | DraftKings (win under 30 min +2); Riot (stomp: under 27 min or 10k gold, +2) | Game length, partly |
| Rate stats at a fixed minute | Riot (CS/min at 15:00, gold lead at 14:00, vision per minute) | Game length |
| Average per weekend | Riot 2025–26 | Series length |
| Fixed or best games only | Riot 2016 ("first two" or "best game"); props ("Maps 1-2") | Series length |
| Sweep bonus | DraftKings (+20 per player) | Series length |

Riot's 2016 change is the closest match to Flol. Leagues chose, at the draft, between "First Two Games", where a third game "is simply ignored", and "Best Game", counting "only the highest-scoring game for each pro". **Riot said Best Game still "favors NA pros who will often have more opportunities to score big"**, because NA played Bo3s ([Riot, archived](https://archive.runeterra.net/na/esports/poroso3m)). Flol's best 2 of a series sits between those two. It has the same tilt: a player whose series goes to three games gets to drop his worst. CBLOL says its averaging "balances out series of different lengths" ([CBLOL rules](https://cblol.gg/pt/rules)). Averaging removes that tilt but gives decimals.

Counting thresholds work against the goal. Riot's 10+ kills (+3) and DraftKings's 10+ K/A (+2) are far easier to reach in a 40-minute game.

## 5. Flol's draft table against the field

Craig's draft: 1 per 3 kills, 1 per 3 assists (supports per 2), −1 per 3 deaths, each capped at 4, plus a CS lead at 15 (vision lead for supports). The examples below assume rounding down and +1 for the lead. Both are open points.

| Game line | Flol draft | DraftKings | Riot LCS (base only) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Carry, 25 min stomp: 6/0/8, 250 CS, CS lead | 2 + 2 + 0 + 1 = **5** | 18 + 16 + 5 = 39 | 9 + 8 + 2.5 = 19.5 |
| Carry, average: 4/2/6, 300 CS, no lead | 1 + 2 + 0 = **3** | 12 − 2 + 12 + 6 = 28 | 6 − 2 + 6 + 3 = 13 |
| Carry, 40 min: 9/3/12, 400 CS, no lead | 3 + 4 − 1 = **6** | 27 − 3 + 24 + 8 + 2 = 58 | 13.5 − 3 + 12 + 4 = 26.5 |
| Support, 40 min: 1/4/16, vision lead | 0 + 4 − 1 + 1 = **4** | 3 − 4 + 32 + 2 = 33 | 1.5 − 4 + 16 = 13.5 |

DraftKings and Riot figures are computed here from their published tables; Riot's role, KP and stomp bonuses are left out.

Four points come out of this. First, **Flol is the only table in whole low numbers**, and the only one with caps. No Riot game capped points per player or per game. Second, the caps bite late. A carry needs 12 kills to hit the kill cap, so in most games the long-game carry still out-scores the stomp carry (6 against 5 above). The assist cap bites sooner for supports, at 8 assists, which is where the cap does the most work. Third, the CS and vision lead at 15 is length-neutral, like Riot's 15:00 rate stats. It is the one line that cannot grow in a long game. Fourth, rounding down throws points away: 2 kills and 2 deaths both score 0. That keeps numbers low but makes the table coarse.

What Flol lacks, compared with the others, is a fast-win term. Every rival that resists long games pays one (DraftKings under 30 min; Riot under 27 min). Flol gives wins to the coach slot, so a quick-win bonus would belong there too, if Craig wants one.

## 6. Steals are recorded per player, free, after the game

**Riot's match-v5 data has `objectivesStolen` and `objectivesStolenAssists` for every player** ([Riot match-v5](https://developer.riotgames.com/api-details/match-v5)). Its `challenges` block adds `epicMonsterSteals`, which matched `objectivesStolen` in the game tested. Pro games sit on tournament realms that a public Riot key cannot reach (unverified). But **Leaguepedia publishes the full match-v5 JSON of pro games, LCK included, as `V5 data:` wiki pages**, readable with no key through its API ([Leaguepedia API](https://lol.fandom.com/api.php)). LCK Cup 2025 and LCK 2025 games were found there with the steal fields present. At Worlds 2026, T1 Oner's record shows 2 steals and Faker's 1 steal assist in one game. The data comes from Riot's esports data portal, which Riot built to support "community use cases, such as Leaguepedia and Oracle's Elixir" ([Riot dev diary](https://lolesports.com/en-US/news/dev-diary-introducing-the-new-lol-esports-data-portal)).

No other source checked has per-player steals. The lolesports live feed gives each team's Dragon types and Baron count only ([community API docs](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/vickz84259/lolesports-api-docs/master/openapi.yaml)). Leaguepedia's Cargo tables and Oracle's Elixir hold team counts only. PandaScore has team objectives and no steals ([PandaScore](https://developers.pandascore.co/docs/data-sample-league-of-legends)). GRID markets "objective control" but names no steal field ([GRID](https://grid.gg/data/games/league-of-legends/)). It is paid, and its schema was not read.

Four limits matter. **Steals can only be scored after the game.** The pages appeared 15 minutes, 2 hours 20 minutes and about 15 hours after three sampled games ended, so they fit a "settled next day" layer. **Riot does not publish what counts as a steal** (which monsters, smite or not), so Flol would take Riot's definition as given. **Steals are rare**: about 2 games in 10 had any in one sample, so a steal bonus is a small, swingy line. And **whether Riot allows a fan game to read this data via Leaguepedia is unknown**. The wiki text is CC BY-SA, but the data is Riot's.

A steal can also be derived as a cross-check. The timeline's monster-kill event has no steal flag. But in the one game tested, the stolen Baron and Dragon listed enemy players among the assists. The rule "a monster kill is a steal when an enemy is in the assist list" matched `objectivesStolen` exactly. It was not tested on grubs, Herald or Atakhan, so it is a check, not the source.

## 7. A late teamfight can be found after the game, not live

The same Leaguepedia pages carry a `/Timeline` with every kill. **Each `CHAMPION_KILL` has a millisecond time, map position, killer, victim and assisting players.** One Worlds 2026 game had 32 such events. Riot even tags kills with `victimTeamfightDamageDealt`, so it tracks a teamfight window internally, but it does not publish the definition ([Riot match-v5](https://developer.riotgames.com/api-details/match-v5)).

There is no standard LoL teamfight. Published methods cluster deaths in time and space. A Dota extractor opens a fight on a death and extends it while more deaths follow within a cooldown ([gem-dota](https://whanyu1212.github.io/gem-dota/reference/extractors/teamfights)). A StarCraft study clusters deaths in x, y and time and drops clusters under 3 deaths ([STARDATA](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1708.02139)). A Flol rule could follow the same shape: after minute X, kills within T seconds and R map units of each other form one fight. A fight with N or more deaths counts, and each player's kills and assists in it score. X, T, R and N would be data Craig tunes.

Live, it is rough. The lolesports feed gives per-player kill and assist counters every half second, but no positions, victims or kill events ([community API docs](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/vickz84259/lolesports-api-docs/master/openapi.yaml)). A time-only cluster is possible live, but it would be provisional until the timeline lands.

## 8. Conclusion

The field splits two ways. Riot fights long games with rates and averages, which forces decimals. DFS and props fight long series by fixing the counted games, and mostly ignore game length. Flol's mix of caps, best 2 games and a 15-minute lead takes the whole-number route no one else has tried. Its weak spots are clear from the comparison. The caps sit too high to stop a long-game carry beating a stomp carry. Best 2 games gives three-game series a free throwaway, which Riot itself flagged in 2016. And there is no fast-win term, which every length-aware rival has.

Steals and a clutch teamfight bonus are both buildable from one free source, Leaguepedia's copy of Riot's match data. Both would settle after the game, not live. Two questions need answers first: whether Riot's data terms let Flol read that copy, and what Riot counts as a steal. Riot's own +4 / +2 steal values give Craig a ready precedent for the size of the bonus.
